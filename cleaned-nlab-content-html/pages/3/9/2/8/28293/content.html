Definition

A category CC is strongly connected if it is inhabited and for any pair of objects A,BA,B there is a morphism A→BA \to B. That is, each hom-set is inhabited.

This terminology is borrowed from graph theory: a directed graph is called strongly connected when it is inhabited and for every two vertices u,vu,v there is a directed path from uu to vv. Hence, a category is strongly connected precisely when its underlying directed graph is strongly connected.

If we merely require that for all pairs of objects A,BA,B there is a morphism A→BA \to B or a morphism B→AB \to A, we call CC semi-strongly connected. This is not standard terminology, though; in fact Bunge 1966 p 16 called this condition strongly connected.

In any case, both these conditions are indeed stronger than that on a connected category, where for each pair of objects there is only required to be a zig-zag of morphisms connecting them.

Properties

  • Every strongly connected category is semi-strongly connected.

  • Every semi-strongly connected category is a connected category.

  • Every connected groupoid is strongly connected.

  • Every inhabited category with zero morphisms is strongly connected.

Examples

Among semi-strongly connected categories are:

  • the category of sets,

  • every inhabited linear order, considered as a thin category.

Among strongly connected categories are:

  • the category of abelian groups,

  • the category of non-empty sets.

Counterexamples

Not semi-strongly connected are:

  • the category of commutative rings,

  • the empty category,

  • the product category Set×SetSet \times Set.
