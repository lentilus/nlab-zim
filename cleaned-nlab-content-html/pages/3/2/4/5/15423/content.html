Definition

In non-archimedean analytic geometry, one considers:

Given a topological space, a quasinet in XX is a set τ={V i}\tau = \{V_i\} of subsets of XX such that for each point x∈Xx\in X there exists a finite number of such subsets V i 1,⋯,V i n∈τV_{i_1}, \cdots, V_{i_n} \in \tau such that

  1. x∈V i 1∩V i 2∩⋯∩V i nx\in V_{i_1} \cap V_{i_2} \cap \cdots \cap V_{i_n} (the point is in their intersection);

  2. V i 1∪⋯∪V i nV_{i_1} \cup \cdots \cup V_{i_n} is a neighbourhood of xx.

(e.g. Berkovich 09, def. 3.1.1.)

A quasinet τ\tau is called a net if for all U,V∈τU,V \in \tau then the restriction τ| U∩V\tau|_{U \cap V} is a quasinet of U∩VU \cap V.

Examples

  • A Berkovich analytic space is a locally Hausdorff topological space equipped with an atlas by affinoid domains such that the underlying analytic spectrum topological spaces of these form a net of compact subsets on XX.
