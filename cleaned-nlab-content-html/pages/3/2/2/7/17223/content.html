Definition

For more context on the following cf. at classifying space.

For n∈ℕn \in \mathbb{N}, write O(n)O(n) for the orthogonal group regarded as a topological group and acting canonically on ℝ n\mathbb{R}^n.

Definition

For k≤N∈ℕk \leq N \in \mathbb{N}, the kkth real Stiefel manifold of ℝ N\mathbb{R}^N is the coset topological space.

(1)V k(ℝ N)≔O(N)/O(N−k), V_k\big(\mathbb{R}^N\big) \;\coloneqq\; O(N)/O(N-k) \,,

where the action of O(N−k)O(N-k) is via the standard subgroup inclusion O(N−k)↪O(N)O(N-k)\hookrightarrow O(N) as an upper right block.

Remark

The canonical group action O(N)↷ℝ NO(N) \curvearrowright \mathbb{R}^N induces a transitive action on the set of kk-dimensional linear subspaces ℝ k⊂ℝ N\mathbb{R}^k \subset \mathbb{R}^N equipped with an orthonormal basis, and given any such subspace WW, then its stabilizer subgroup in O(N)O(N) is isomorphic to O(N−k)O(N-k), the symmetries of the orthogonal subspace W ⊥W^\perp. In this way the underlying set of V k(ℝ N)V_k(\mathbb{R}^N) is in natural bijection to the set of kk-dimensional linear subspaces in ℝ N\mathbb{R}^N equipped with orthonormal bases. The realization of this set as a coset (1) serves to equip it naturally with the structure of a topological space.

More generally, given any finite-dimensional inner product space (V,⟨,⟩)(V,\langle\ ,\ \rangle), there is a corresponding orthogonal group O(V)O(V), and one can repeat the above definition more or less verbatim.

Definition

By def. there are canonical inclusions V k(ℝ N)↪V k(ℝ N+1)V_k(\mathbb{R}^N) \hookrightarrow V_k(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}) that are compatible with the actions of the respective orthogonal groups. The colimit (in Top, see there) over these inclusions is denoted

EO(k)≔lim⟶ NV k(ℝ N). E O(k) \;\coloneqq\; \underset{\longrightarrow}{\lim}_N V_k(\mathbb{R}^N) \,.

This is a model for the total space of the O(k)O(k)-universal principal bundle.

CW-complex structure

Proposition

The Stiefel manifold V k(ℝ N)V_k(\mathbb{R}^N) admits the structure] of a CW-complex.

(e.g. James 1959 p. 3, James 1976 p. 5 with p. 21, Hatcher 2002 p. 302, Blaszczyk 2007)

And it should be true that with that cell structure the inclusions V k(ℝ N)↪V k(ℝ N+1)V_k(\mathbb{R}^N) \hookrightarrow V_k(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}) are subcomplex inclusions:

According to Yokota 1956, the inclusions SU(k)↪SU(N)SU(k)\hookrightarrow SU(N) are cellular such that this is compatible with the group action (reviewed here in 3.3 and 3.3.1). This implies that also the projection SU(N)→SU(N)/SU(N−k)SU(N) \to SU(N)/SU(N-k) is cellular (e.g. Hatcher 2002 p. 302).

Relation to Grassmannians and universal bundles

Similarly, the Grassmannian manifold is the coset

Gr k(ℝ N)≔O(N)/(O(k)×O(N−k)). Gr_k(\mathbb{R}^N) \;\coloneqq\; O(N)/(O(k)\times O(N-k)) \,.

The quotient coprojection

V k(ℝ N)⟶Gr k(ℝ N) V_k(\mathbb{R}^N) \longrightarrow Gr_k(\mathbb{R}^N)

is an O(k)O(k)-principal bundle, with associated bundle V k(ℝ N)× O(k)ℝ kV_k(\mathbb{R}^N) \times_{O(k)} \mathbb{R}^k a vector bundle of rank kk. In the limit (colimit) that N→∞N \to \infty this gives a presentation of the O(k)O(k)-universal principal bundle and of the universal vector bundle of rank kk, respectively. The base space Gr k(∞)≃ wheBO(k)Gr_k(\infty)\simeq_{whe} B O(k) is the classifying space for O(k)O(k)-principal bundles and for rank kk vector bundles.
