Idea

An embedding is a morphism which, in some sense, is an isomorphism onto its image.

For this to make sense in a given category CC, we not only need a good notion of images. Note that it is not enough to have the image of f:X→Yf\colon X \to Y as a subobject imf\im f of YY; we also need to be able to interpret ff as a morphism from XX to imf\im f, because it is this morphism that we are asking to be an isomorphism.

  • effective epimorphism⇒\Rightarrow regular epimorphism ⇔\Leftrightarrow covering

  • effective monomorphism⇒\Rightarrow regular monomorphism ⇔\Leftrightarrow embedding .

As regular or effective monomorphisms

Definition

One general abstract way to define an embedding morphism is to say that this is equivalently a regular monomorphism.

If the ambient category has finite limits and finite colimits, then this is equivalently an effective monomorphism. In terms of this, we recover a formalization of the above idea, that an embedding is an isomorphism onto its image :

For a morphism f:X→Yf : X \to Y in CC, the definition of an image as an equalizer says that the image of ff is

imf≔lim ←(Y→→Y∐ XY). im f \coloneqq \lim_\leftarrow ( Y \stackrel{\to}{\to} Y \coprod_X Y) \,.

In particular, we have a factorization of ff as

f:X→f˜imf↪Y, f \colon X \stackrel{\tilde f}{\to} im f \hookrightarrow Y \,,

where the morphism on the right is a monomorphism.

The morphism ff being an effective monomorphism means that f˜\tilde f is an isomorphism, and hence that ff is an “isomomorphism onto its image”.

Examples

In TopTop

A morphism U→XU \to X of topological spaces is a regular monomorphism precisely if it is an injection such that the topology on UU is the induced topology. This is an embedding of topological spaces.
