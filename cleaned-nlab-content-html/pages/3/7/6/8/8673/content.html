Definition

A finite abelian group is a group which is both finite and abelian.

Properties

As any finite group, a finite abelian group is pure torsion.

Proposition

If a finite abelian group AA has order |A|=p{\vert A \vert} = p a prime number, then it is the cyclic group ℤ p\mathbb{Z}_p.

Proposition

If AA is a finite abelian group and p∈ℕp \in \mathbb{N} is a prime number that divides the order |A|{\vert A \vert}, then equivalently

  • AA has an element of order pp;

  • AA has a subgroup of order pp.

This is Cauchy's theorem restricted to abelian groups.

Proof

We prodeed by induction on the order of AA. For |A|=2{\vert A \vert} = 2 we have that A=ℤ 2A = \mathbb{Z}_2 is the unique group of order 2 and the statement holds for p=2p =2.

Assume then that the statement has been show for groups of order <n\lt n and let |A|=n{\vert A \vert} = n.

If AA has no non-trivial proper subgroup then nn must be prime and A=ℤ nA = \mathbb{Z}_n a cyclic group and the statement follows.

If AA does have a non-trivial proper subgroup H↪AH \hookrightarrow A then pp divides either |H|{\vert H \vert} or |A/H|\vert A/H\vert.

In the first case by induction assumption HH has an element of order pp which is therefore also an element of GG of order pp.

In the second case there is by induction assumption an element a∈Aa \in A such that a+H∈A/Ha + H \in A/H has order pp. Since the order of a+H∈A/Ha + H \in A/H divides the order of a∈Aa \in A it follows that aa has order kpk p for some k∈ℕk \in \mathbb{N}. Then kak a has order pp.

Theorem

(fundamental theorem of finite abelian groups)

Every finite abelian group is the direct sum of cyclic groups of prime power order (its p-primary groups).

See for instance Navarro 2003, Sullivan 2003.
