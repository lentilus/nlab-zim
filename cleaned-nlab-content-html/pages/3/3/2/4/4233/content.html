conormal sheaf

Given a closed immersion of schemes f=(f,f ♯):(Y,O Y)→(X,O X)f= (f,f^\sharp): (Y,O_Y)\to (X,O_X) of (or of more general locally ringed spaces, e.g. of analytic varieties), the kernel ℐ=O X\mathcal{I} = O_X of the comorphism f ♯:O X→f *O Yf^\sharp:O_X\to f_* O_Y is a sheaf of ideals, called the defining sheaf of the closed immersion ff.

The conormal sheaf is the quotient sheaf? of OO-modules given by Ω X/Y:=ℐ/ℐ 2\Omega_{X/Y} := \mathcal{I}/\mathcal{I}^2. In the algebraic setting, it is also called the sheaf of relative Kähler differentials. It is always quasicoherent. Most important example is the case of the diagonal Δ:X→X× SX\Delta:X\to X\times_S X of an SS-scheme XX. For example, when S=SpeckS = Spec k and X=SpecRX = Spec R where RR is a kk-algebra then the global sections of Ω X× SX/X\Omega_{X\times_S X/X} form the kernel of the multiplication map R× kR→RR\times_k R\to R quotiented by its square.

One sometimes says conormal bundle meaning a conormal sheaf, though the bundle and sheaf are two points of view in the cases when the conormal sheaf is locally free of constant rank.
