Idea

A normed ring is a ring compatibly equipped with a norm on the underlying abelian group.

If this is suitably complete with respect to the norm, then a normed ring is called a Banach ring. A normed ring which is a field is, naturally, called a normed field, and if the norm is multiplicative it is also called a valued field.

The Berkovich spectrum of a normed ring RR is the set of multiplicative seminorms on RR that are bounded by the norm on RR.

Definition

Definition

A normed ring is a ring RR equipped with a function

|−|:R⟶ℝ ≥0 {\vert -\vert} \;\colon\; R \longrightarrow \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}

to the non-negative real numbers, such that for all f,g∈Rf,g \in R:

  1. |f|=0{\vert f \vert} = 0 precisely if f=0f = 0;

  2. |−f|=|f|\vert -f\vert = \vert f \vert

  3. |f+g|≤|f|+|g|{\vert f + g \vert} \leq {\vert f \vert}+ {\vert g \vert} (triangle identity)

  4. |f⋅g|≤|f|⋅|g|{\vert f \cdot g\vert} \leq {\vert f \vert\cdot {\vert g \vert}}.

(cf. Ozaki, Kashiwagi & Tsuboi 1953)

Remark

One may also define a normed ring to be a monoid internal to the monoidal category NGrpNGrp of normed groups. If the morphisms in NGrpNGrp are taken to be the short group homomorphisms and the projective cross norm is used on the tensor product, then this reproduces the definition above. If (as is often seen) the morphisms are generalized to bounded group homomorphisms, then this generalizes the third clause in def. to

  • there is C∈ℝ >0C \in \mathbb{R}_{\gt 0} such that for all f,g∈Rf,g \in R

    |f⋅g|≤C⋅|f|⋅|g| {\vert f \cdot g\vert} \leq C \cdot {\vert f \vert\cdot {\vert g \vert}}

(cf. Bassat & Kremnitzer 2013, remark 6.32)

Examples

Example

A normed field is of course in particular a normed ring.

Example

For RR a normed commutative ring, then for each n∈ℕn \in \mathbb{N} the matrix algebra Mat n(R)Mat_n(R) becomes a normed ring with norm

|A|≔∑ i,j=1 n|A i,j|.{\vert A\vert} \coloneqq \sum_{i,j=1}^n {\vert A_{i,j}\vert} \,.

Alternatively, if RR is strictly a non-Archimedean normed ring, the max norm |A|≔max 1≤i,j≤n(|A i,j|){\vert A\vert} \coloneqq \max_{1 \leq i,j \leq n}({\vert A_{i,j}\vert}) is also submultiplicative.

Notice that:

  • even if the norm on RR is multiplicative (is an absolute value), the induced norm on Mat n(R)Mat_n(R) is not in general multiplicative;

  • if RR is a Banach ring, then so is Mat n(R)Mat_n(R).

(cf. Jarden 2011).
