Idea

The projective unitary group on an infinite-dimensional separable complex Hilbert space ℋ\mathcal{H} is traditionally denoted PU(ℋ)PU(\mathcal{H}), being the quotient of the unitary group U(ℋ) by its circle subgroup S 1≃S(ℂ)≃S^1 \simeq S(\mathbb{C}) \simeq U(1).

Properties

General

Proposition

The U(1)-quotient space coprojection of U(ℋ) over PU(ℋ) – both in their strong operator topology – is a principal U(1)-bundle:

S 1 ↪ U(ℋ) ↓ PU(ℋ)≃U(ℋ)/S 1 \array{ S^1 &\hookrightarrow& \mathrm{U}(\mathcal{H}) \\ && \big\downarrow \\ && PU(\mathcal{H}) \mathrlap{ \; \simeq \; \mathrm{U}(\mathcal{H})/S^1 } }

(Simms 1970, Thm. 1)
Remark

Prop. means in particular that U(ℋ)→PU(ℋ)\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{H}) \xrightarrow{\;} PU(\mathcal{H}) is locally trivial, hence that the coset space coprojection U(ℋ)→U(ℋ)/S 1\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{H}) \xrightarrow{\;} \mathrm{U}(\mathcal{H})/S^1 admits local sections. See also at coset space coprojection admitting local sections.

Proposition

In its operator topology (here), PU(ℋ)PU(\mathcal{H}) is a well-pointed topological group.

(Hebestreit & Sagave 2020, p. 23, using Dardalat & Pennig 2016, Prop. 2.26)

Homomorphisms into PU(ℋ)PU(\mathcal{H})

Since Γ≔\Gamma \,\coloneqq\, PU(ℋ) is not a (finite-dimensional) Lie group, it falls outside the applicability of the general theorem that nearby homomorphisms from compact Lie groups are conjugate. Nevertheless, the conclusion still holds, at least for domain GG a discrete, hence finite group:

The PU(ℋ)-space of homomorphisms Hom(G,PU(ℋ))Hom\big(G,\, PU(\mathcal{H})\big) is a disjoint union (as here) of orbits of the conjugation action.

This is established in Uribe & Lück 2013, Sec. 15, p. 38.
