Idea

The Waldhausen S •S_\bullet-construction (Waldhausen 85, §1.3) is a procedure that produces algebraic K-theory (as an infinite loop space or connective spectrum) from a category or ( ∞ , 1 ) (\infty,1) -category 𝒞\mathcal{C} equipped with a notion of cofiber sequences.

In degree nn, the simplicial object S •𝒞S_\bullet \mathcal{C} consists of length-nn filtrations

0↪A 1↪A 2↪⋯↪A n 0 \hookrightarrow A_1 \hookrightarrow A_2 \hookrightarrow \cdots \hookrightarrow A_n

together with choices of all their subquotients A j/A iA_j/A_i. Hence the geometric realization |S •𝒞|\left\vert S_\bullet \mathcal{C} \right\vert (of the core or of the subcategory of weak equivalences, see below) has a single vertex, one edge for each object AA of 𝒞\mathcal{C}, and one 2-simplex for each cofiber sequence A↪B↠B/AA \hookrightarrow B \twoheadrightarrow B/A, whose three edges are AA, BB and B/AB/A. Therefore, in the fundamental group of |S •𝒞|\left\vert S_\bullet \mathcal{C}\right\vert the relation

[B]=[A]+[B/A] [B] \,=\, [A] + [B/A]

holds, which is the defining relation of the Grothendieck group K 0(𝒞)K_0(\mathcal{C}), while the higher simplices coherently relate these relations. Accordingly, the algebraic K-theory space of 𝒞\mathcal{C} is the loop space

K(𝒞)≔Ω|S •𝒞|, K(\mathcal{C}) \;\coloneqq\; \Omega \left\vert S_\bullet \mathcal{C} \right\vert \,,

whose homotopy groups K n(𝒞)≔π nK(𝒞)=π n+1|S •𝒞|K_n(\mathcal{C}) \coloneqq \pi_n K(\mathcal{C}) = \pi_{n+1} \left\vert S_\bullet \mathcal{C} \right\vert are the algebraic K-groups of 𝒞\mathcal{C}, starting with K 0(𝒞)≃π 1|S •𝒞|K_0(\mathcal{C}) \simeq \pi_1 \left\vert S_\bullet \mathcal{C} \right\vert (Weibel 13, Prop. IV.8.4).

Waldhausen describes the passage from 𝒞\mathcal{C} to Ω|S •𝒞|\Omega \left\vert S_\bullet \mathcal{C} \right\vert as reminiscent of, and in part motivated by, the group completion of Graeme Segal‘s Γ \Gamma -space machine (Segal 74). The difference is that it imposes additivity over all cofiber sequences, not just over direct sums (Waldhausen 85, §1.3, §1.8).

The construction can take as input any of the following kinds of data:

  • Waldhausen category (below)

  • Waldhausen ( ∞ , 1 ) (\infty,1) -category (Barwick 16)

  • stable ( ∞ , 1 ) (\infty,1) -category (below)

  • stable derivator (Maltsiniotis 07, Garkusha 07, Muro & Raptis 17)

Definition

For Waldhausen categories

For n∈ℕn \in \mathbb{N}, write Ar[n]≔Fun([1],[n])\mathrm{Ar}[n] \coloneqq \mathrm{Fun}\big([1],[n]\big) for the arrow category of [n]={0<1<⋯<n}[n] = \{0 \lt 1 \lt \cdots \lt n\}, hence for the poset of pairs (i,j)(i,j) with 0≤i≤j≤n0 \leq i \leq j \leq n, where (i,j)≤(i′,j′)(i,j) \leq (i',j') iff i≤i′i \leq i' and j≤j′j \leq j'.

Definition

(S-construction of a Waldhausen category)
Let 𝒞\mathcal{C} be a Waldhausen category, with zero object 00, cofibrations denoted ↪\hookrightarrow, and subcategory of weak equivalences w𝒞\mathrm{w}\mathcal{C}.

  1. S n𝒞S_n \mathcal{C} is the category whose objects are the functors

    A:Ar[n]⟶𝒞,(i,j)↦A i,j A \,\colon\, \mathrm{Ar}[n] \longrightarrow \mathcal{C} \,, \;\;\;\;\; (i,j) \mapsto A_{i,j}

    such that

    • A j,j=0A_{j,j} = 0 for all jj,

    • for all i≤j≤ki \leq j \leq k the morphism A i,j→A i,kA_{i,j} \to A_{i,k} is a cofibration and the square

      A i,j ↪ A i,k ↓ ↓ 0=A j,j ↪ A j,k \array{ A_{i,j} &\hookrightarrow& A_{i,k} \\ \big\downarrow && \big\downarrow \\ 0 = A_{j,j} &\hookrightarrow& A_{j,k} }

      is a pushout, hence A i,j↪A i,k↠A j,kA_{i,j} \hookrightarrow A_{i,k} \twoheadrightarrow A_{j,k} is a cofiber sequence,

    and whose morphisms are the natural transformations between these.

  2. wS n𝒞⊂S n𝒞\mathrm{w}S_n \mathcal{C} \subset S_n \mathcal{C} is the subcategory of those natural transformations A→A′A \to A' all of whose components A i,j→A′ i,jA_{i,j} \to A'_{i,j} are weak equivalences.

Since [n]↦Ar[n][n] \mapsto \mathrm{Ar}[n] is a cosimplicial poset, these categories form simplicial objects S •𝒞S_\bullet \mathcal{C} and wS •𝒞\mathrm{w}S_\bullet \mathcal{C} in Cat.

(Waldhausen 85, §1.3, Weibel 13, Def. IV.8.3)

Remark

(explicit description)
An object of S n𝒞S_n \mathcal{C} is equivalently a sequence of cofibrations

A 0,1↪A 0,2↪⋯↪A 0,n A_{0,1} \hookrightarrow A_{0,2} \hookrightarrow \cdots \hookrightarrow A_{0,n}

together with a choice of all quotients A i,j≃A 0,j/A 0,iA_{i,j} \simeq A_{0,j}/A_{0,i}, as shown here for n=3n = 3:

A 0,1 ↪ A 0,2 ↪ A 0,3 ↓ ↓ A 1,2 ↪ A 1,3 ↓ A 2,3 \array{ A_{0,1} &\hookrightarrow& A_{0,2} &\hookrightarrow& A_{0,3} \\ && \big\downarrow && \big\downarrow \\ && A_{1,2} &\hookrightarrow& A_{1,3} \\ && && \big\downarrow \\ && && A_{2,3} }

In these terms (Waldhausen 85, §1.3):

  • for 0<i≤n0 \lt i \leq n, the face map d i:S n𝒞→S n−1𝒞d_i \colon S_n\mathcal{C} \to S_{n-1}\mathcal{C} drops A 0,iA_{0,i} from the sequence,

  • the face map d 0d_0 is “quotient by A 0,1A_{0,1}”, replacing the sequence by A 1,2↪⋯↪A 1,nA_{1,2} \hookrightarrow \cdots \hookrightarrow A_{1,n}.

In low degrees:

  • S 0𝒞S_0 \mathcal{C} is the trivial category (one object, one morphism), so that |wS 0𝒞|=*\left\vert \mathrm{w}S_0 \mathcal{C} \right\vert = \ast,

  • S 1𝒞≃𝒞S_1 \mathcal{C} \simeq \mathcal{C},

  • S 2𝒞S_2 \mathcal{C} is the category of cofiber sequences A 0,1↪A 0,2↠A 1,2A_{0,1} \hookrightarrow A_{0,2} \twoheadrightarrow A_{1,2}, and the three face maps S 2𝒞→S 1𝒞≃𝒞S_2 \mathcal{C} \to S_1 \mathcal{C} \simeq \mathcal{C} send such a sequence to d 0=A 1,2d_0 = A_{1,2}, d 1=A 0,2d_1 = A_{0,2} and d 2=A 0,1d_2 = A_{0,1}, respectively.

Moreover, each S n𝒞S_n \mathcal{C} is itself a Waldhausen category, so that S •𝒞S_\bullet \mathcal{C} is a simplicial Waldhausen category (Waldhausen 85, §1.3).

Definition

(Waldhausen K-theory)
The algebraic K-theory space of a Waldhausen category 𝒞\mathcal{C} is the loop space

K(𝒞)≔Ω|wS •𝒞| K(\mathcal{C}) \;\coloneqq\; \Omega \left\vert \mathrm{w}S_\bullet \mathcal{C} \right\vert

of the geometric realization of the bisimplicial set ([n],[m])↦N m(wS n𝒞)\big([n],[m]\big) \mapsto N_m\big(\mathrm{w}S_n \mathcal{C}\big) (degreewise nerve), based at its unique vertex. Its homotopy groups are the K-groups K n(𝒞)≔π nK(𝒞)=π n+1|wS •𝒞|K_n(\mathcal{C}) \coloneqq \pi_n K(\mathcal{C}) = \pi_{n+1} \left\vert \mathrm{w}S_\bullet \mathcal{C} \right\vert.

(Waldhausen 85, §1.3, Weibel 13, Def. IV.8.5)

Remark

(K-theory spectrum)
Since S •𝒞S_\bullet \mathcal{C} is a simplicial Waldhausen category, the construction can be iterated, giving multisimplicial categories wS • (n)𝒞≔wS •⋯S •𝒞\mathrm{w}S^{(n)}_\bullet \mathcal{C} \coloneqq \mathrm{w}S_\bullet \cdots S_\bullet \mathcal{C} (nn-fold). In each new S •S_\bullet-direction the 1-skeleton is the suspension of the previous stage (by S 0=*S_0 = \ast and S 1≃idS_1 \simeq \mathrm{id}), and the resulting inclusions Σ|wS • (n)𝒞|↪|wS • (n+1)𝒞|\Sigma \left\vert \mathrm{w}S^{(n)}_\bullet \mathcal{C} \right\vert \hookrightarrow \left\vert \mathrm{w}S^{(n+1)}_\bullet \mathcal{C} \right\vert make the sequence of spaces

K(𝒞) n≔|wS • (n)𝒞| \mathbf{K}(\mathcal{C})_n \;\coloneqq\; \left\vert \mathrm{w}S^{(n)}_\bullet \mathcal{C} \right\vert

a sequential spectrum, whose nnth space is (n−1)(n-1)-connected. By the additivity theorem, this is an Ω \Omega -spectrum beyond the zeroth term: the adjoint structure maps K(𝒞) n→ΩK(𝒞) n+1\mathbf{K}(\mathcal{C})_n \to \Omega\, \mathbf{K}(\mathcal{C})_{n+1} are weak equivalences for n≥1n \geq 1 (Waldhausen 85, §1.3 with Prop. 1.5.3). Hence K(𝒞)≃Ω ∞K(𝒞)K(\mathcal{C}) \simeq \Omega^\infty \mathbf{K}(\mathcal{C}) is an infinite loop space, and the connective spectrum K(𝒞)\mathbf{K}(\mathcal{C}) is the algebraic K-theory spectrum of 𝒞\mathcal{C}.

The first map |w𝒞|→Ω|wS •𝒞|\left\vert \mathrm{w}\mathcal{C} \right\vert \to \Omega \left\vert \mathrm{w}S_\bullet \mathcal{C} \right\vert is in general not an equivalence; it is the K-theoretic analogue of a group completion.

For stable (∞,1)(\infty,1)-categories

For 𝒞\mathcal{C} a stable ( ∞ , 1 ) (\infty,1) -category (or more generally a pointed (∞,1)(\infty,1)-category with finite colimits, Lurie 14, Lec. 16), all morphisms serve as cofibrations and the equivalences as weak equivalences, so that the above definition becomes:

Definition

(S-construction of a stable (∞,1)(\infty,1)-category)
S n𝒞⊂Fun(Ar[n],𝒞)S_n \mathcal{C} \subset \mathrm{Fun}\big(\mathrm{Ar}[n], \mathcal{C}\big) is the full sub-(∞,1)(\infty,1)-category on those functors AA with A j,j≃0A_{j,j} \simeq 0 for all jj and such that all squares as above are pushouts (equivalently, in the stable case, pullbacks). The algebraic K-theory space of 𝒞\mathcal{C} is

K(𝒞)≔Ω|(S •𝒞) ≃|, K(\mathcal{C}) \;\coloneqq\; \Omega \left\vert \big(S_\bullet \mathcal{C}\big)^{\simeq} \right\vert \,,

where (−) ≃(-)^\simeq denotes the core and |−|{\vert-\vert} the geometric realization ( ( ∞ , 1 ) (\infty,1) -colimit) of the resulting simplicial ∞ \infty -groupoid.

(BGT 13, §7.1, Barwick 16, Lurie 14, Lec. 16)

As in this Remark, restriction to the first row, A↦(A 0,1→A 0,2→⋯→A 0,n)A \mapsto \big(A_{0,1} \to A_{0,2} \to \cdots \to A_{0,n}\big), is an equivalence

S n𝒞≃Fun(Δ n−1,𝒞), S_n \mathcal{C} \;\simeq\; \mathrm{Fun}\big(\Delta^{n-1}, \mathcal{C}\big) \,,

since all the quotients are determined up to contractible choice (Lurie 14, Lec. 16, Rem. 6). For Waldhausen categories with suitable models, Waldhausen K-theory agrees with the K-theory of the underlying (∞,1)(\infty,1)-category (BGT 13, §7.2).

Remark

(relation to the nerve)
Beware that (S •𝒞) ≃(S_\bullet \mathcal{C})^\simeq is not the complete Segal space [n]↦Fun(Δ n,𝒞) ≃[n] \mapsto \mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^n, \mathcal{C})^\simeq of 𝒞\mathcal{C}. The geometric realization of the latter is the classifying space |𝒞|\left\vert \mathcal{C} \right\vert of 𝒞\mathcal{C} (the ∞\infty-groupoid obtained by inverting all morphisms), which is contractible as soon as 𝒞\mathcal{C} has an initial object or terminal object, such as a zero object.

Instead, the two are related by a shift of degrees: by the above equivalence, S n+1𝒞≃Fun(Δ n,𝒞)S_{n+1} \mathcal{C} \simeq \mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^n, \mathcal{C}), compatibly with all face and degeneracy maps except d 0d_0 and s 0s_0 of S •𝒞S_\bullet \mathcal{C}. This means that the complete Segal space of 𝒞\mathcal{C} is the décalage (“path object”) P(S •𝒞) ≃P(S_\bullet \mathcal{C})^\simeq of (S •𝒞) ≃(S_\bullet \mathcal{C})^\simeq, which discards d 0d_0 and s 0s_0 (Lurie 14, Lec. 16, Rem. 8). Generally, the décalage PX •P X_\bullet of a simplicial object is simplicially homotopy equivalent to the constant simplicial object on X 0X_0 (Waldhausen 85, Lemma 1.5.1). Here X 0=(S 0𝒞) ≃≃*X_0 = (S_0 \mathcal{C})^\simeq \simeq \ast, consistent with |𝒞|≃*\left\vert \mathcal{C} \right\vert \simeq \ast.

So the K-theoretic information sits in the face map d 0d_0, which is invisible to the nerve. It sends a sequence A 0,1→A 0,2→⋯→A 0,nA_{0,1} \to A_{0,2} \to \cdots \to A_{0,n} to the sequence A 0,2/A 0,1→⋯→A 0,n/A 0,1A_{0,2}/A_{0,1} \to \cdots \to A_{0,n}/A_{0,1} of quotients by its first term. The canonical map 𝒞 ≃→K(𝒞)\mathcal{C}^\simeq \to K(\mathcal{C}) arises from the sequence (S 1𝒞) ≃→|P(S •𝒞) ≃|≃*→|(S •𝒞) ≃|(S_1\mathcal{C})^\simeq \to \left\vert P(S_\bullet \mathcal{C})^\simeq \right\vert \simeq \ast \to \left\vert (S_\bullet \mathcal{C})^\simeq \right\vert (Waldhausen 85, §1.5).

Properties

Additivity

Proposition

(additivity theorem)
For 𝒞\mathcal{C} a Waldhausen category, the functor S 2𝒞⟶𝒞×𝒞S_2 \mathcal{C} \longrightarrow \mathcal{C} \times \mathcal{C} which sends a cofiber sequence A↪B↠B/AA \hookrightarrow B \twoheadrightarrow B/A to the pair (A,B/A)(A, B/A) induces a homotopy equivalence

|wS •(S 2𝒞)|⟶∼|wS •𝒞|×|wS •𝒞|. \left\vert \mathrm{w}S_\bullet \big(S_2 \mathcal{C}\big) \right\vert \overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow} \left\vert \mathrm{w}S_\bullet \mathcal{C} \right\vert \times \left\vert \mathrm{w}S_\bullet \mathcal{C} \right\vert \,.

Equivalently, for every cofiber sequence F′→F→F″F' \to F \to F'' of exact functors, the map |wS •F|\left\vert \mathrm{w}S_\bullet F \right\vert is homotopic to |wS •(F′∨F″)|\left\vert \mathrm{w}S_\bullet (F' \vee F'') \right\vert, where F′∨F″F' \vee F'' is the objectwise coproduct of functors.

(Waldhausen 85, Prop. 1.3.2, Thm. 1.4.2; for (∞,1)(\infty,1)-categories: Lurie 14, Lec. 17, Barwick 16)

The additivity theorem is what makes K(𝒞)\mathbf{K}(\mathcal{C}) an Ω\Omega-spectrum (this Remark). In the universal characterization of connective K-theory as the universal additive invariant of stable (∞,1)(\infty,1)-categories, the S •S_\bullet-construction becomes the suspension in the (∞,1)(\infty,1)-category of additive noncommutative motives (BGT 13, §7).

Comparison to other constructions

  • For an exact category 𝒜\mathcal{A}, regarded as a Waldhausen category with cofibrations the admissible monomorphisms and weak equivalences the isomorphisms, there is a natural homotopy equivalence |iS •𝒜|≃|Q𝒜|\left\vert \mathrm{i}S_\bullet \mathcal{A} \right\vert \simeq \left\vert Q \mathcal{A} \right\vert with Quillen’s Q-construction. Hence Waldhausen K-theory agrees with Quillen K-theory in this case (Waldhausen 85, §1.9, Weibel 13, IV.8.6).

  • For the comparison with K-theory via Segal’s group completion in the case of split cofibrations, see Waldhausen 85, §1.8.

Higher structure

  • S •𝒞S_\bullet \mathcal{C} is a 2-Segal space (but in general not a Segal space), and as such it underlies the construction of Hall algebras (Dyckerhoff & Kapranov 19).

  • For 𝒞\mathcal{C} stable, S •𝒞S_\bullet \mathcal{C} refines to a paracyclic object, which yields “rotation invariance” of K-theory: the K-theory functor is equivariant with respect to a S 1 S^1 -action on stable (∞,1)(\infty,1)-categories (and the trivial action on spaces) under which the generator of π 1(S 1)\pi_1(S^1) acts by double suspension Σ 2\Sigma^2 (Lurie, Rotation invariance). Related cyclic symmetries are discussed in Nadler 15.

  • Under a categorified Dold-Kan correspondence, S •𝒞S_\bullet \mathcal{C} is the categorified nerve of the chain complex of stable (∞,1)(\infty,1)-categories given by 𝒞\mathcal{C} concentrated in degree 1 (Dyckerhoff 21, Intro. and Ex. 3.27). This is in analogy to how, under the ordinary Dold-Kan correspondence, an abelian group in degree 1 corresponds to its bar construction.
