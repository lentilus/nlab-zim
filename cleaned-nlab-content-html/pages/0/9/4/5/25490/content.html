Idea

In homotopy theory, by an ℐ\mathcal{I}-space [Sagave & Schlichtkrull (2011)] one means a functor ℐ→𝒮\mathcal{I} \to \mathcal{S} from

  1. the category ℐ\mathcal{I} of finite sets (including the empty set) with injective maps between them;

  2. a category such as TopologicalSpaces or SimplicialSets which carries a classical model category structure presenting plain homotopy types.

The functor category 𝒮 ℐ\mathcal{S}^{\mathcal{I}} of ℐ\mathcal{I}-spaces then carries a model category-structure whose weak equivalences are those natural transformations which induce weak homotopy equivalences on homotopy colimits over ℐ\mathcal{I}; and this turns out to be Quillen equivalent to the classical model structure via the colimit-functor

As such, ℐ\mathcal{I}-spaces are just another presentation of homotopy theory.

However, via Day convolution of the evident symmetric monoidal category-structure on ℐ\mathcal{I} (whose tensor product is disjoint union of finite sets) the functor category 𝒮 ℐ\mathcal{S}^{\mathcal{I}} of ℐ\mathcal{I}-spaces becomes itself symmetric monoidal.

This monoidal structure on ℐ\mathcal{I}-spaces is more homotopy-truthful than the (cartesian) monoidal structure on plain spaces: strictly commutative monoids internal to ℐ\mathcal{I}-spaces serve to model all E ∞ E_\infty -spaces.
