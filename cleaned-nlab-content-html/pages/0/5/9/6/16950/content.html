Idea

A square matrix (A x,y)∈Mat n×n(R)(A_{x,y})\in Mat_{n \times n}(R) whose values for x≠yx\neq y are all zero, hence whose entries are concentrated on the diagonal, is called a diagonal matrix.

The diagonal matrices form a non-central commutative subalgebra of the matrix algebra Mat n×n(R)Mat_{n \times n}(R), isomorphic to the nnfold direct sum of the ground ring RR.

Examples

A diagonal matrix with value 1 in all diagonal entries is an identity matrix.
