Idea

There exists exotic smooth structure on the Euclidean space ℝ 4\mathbb{R}^4 of dimension 4.

(For all other dimensions there is a unique smooth structure, the standard one, see at smooth structure).

Construction

Construction from the failure of smooth surgery theory

Exotic,ℝ 4\phantom{,}\mathbb{R}^4s, i.e. smooth manifolds homeomorphic but not diffeomorphic to ℝ 4\mathbb{R}^4, may be understood by comparison with exotic 7-spheres such as the Milnor spheres. In both cases there are closed topological nn-manifolds MM (n=8n = 8 resp. 44) that admit no smooth structure, but become smoothable once a closed nn-ball is removed. The failure lies in gluing the ball back in. The conclusions differ, though. For n=8n = 8, a neighbourhood of the removed ball can be taken to be diffeomorphic to Σ×ℝ\Sigma \times \mathbb{R}, where Σ\Sigma is homeomorphic to S 7 S^7 but not diffeomorphic to it, since otherwise a smooth disc could be glued in along Σ\Sigma. So Σ\Sigma is an exotic 7-sphere. For n=4n = 4 this cannot happen, since S 3 S^3 has no exotic smooth structure (by Moise's theorem, (Sco. 05, p. 101)). Instead the obstruction lies in an exotic smooth structure on the 4-dimensional collar S 3×ℝ≅ℝ 4∖{0}S^3 \times \mathbb{R} \cong \mathbb{R}^4 \setminus \{0\} itself. This is a manifestation of the failure of smooth surgery theory in dimension 4, which also produces exotic ℝ 4\mathbb{R}^4s.

Construction from the K3 surface

A concrete construction is the K3 surface K3K3, described by the homogenous equation x 4+y 4+z 4+w 4=0x^4+y^4+z^4+w^4=0 in twistor space ℂP 3\mathbb{C}P^3. (Fre. & Uhl. 84, p. 21, Sco. 05, p. 130) According to Freedman's classification, (Fre. & Uhl. 84, p. 22-24, Sco. 05, p. 240-243) it is homeomorphic to the connected sum 2M E 8¯#3(S 2×S 2)2\overline{M_{E_8}}\#3(S^2\times S^2) of twice the orientation-reversed E8 manifold M E 8M_{E_8} and thrice the complex surface S 2×S 2≅ℂP 1×ℂP 1S^2\times S^2\cong\mathbb{C}P^1\times\mathbb{C}P^1 gained from the product of the Riemann sphere with itself. Although K3K3 is a smooth 4-manifold, 2M E 8¯#k(S 2×S 2)2\overline{M_{E_8}}\#k(S^2\times S^2) isn’t for k=0,1,2k=0,1,2. For k=0k=0, this follows from Donaldson's theorem, (Fre. & Uhl. 84, p. 22-24, Sco. 05, p. 243) and for k=1,2k=1,2, it was also shown by Simon Donaldson, (Sco. 05, p. 248) although Donaldson's theorem itself no longer applies. (A simpler argument was later obtained with Seiberg-Witten invariants.) Hence a smooth surgery along the connecting sphere S 3S^3 to remove one, two or three S 2×S 2S^2\times S^2 from the smooth K3 surface K3K3 must fail. (Fre. & Uhl. 84, p. 24-30) (It is an open question whether this also holds for any connected sum of the K3 surface K3K3, which is known as 11/8 conjecture.) In the simplest surgery of removing three S 2×S 2S^2\times S^2, the failure spawns an exotic R^4 in #3(S 2×S 2)\#3(S^2\times S^2).

Construction from a nine-fold blow-up

The intersection form of the smooth complex surface ℂP 2#9ℂP 2¯\mathbb{C}P^2\#9\overline{\mathbb{C}P^2}, the simplest example of an elliptic surface known as Dolgachev surface, is [+1]⊕9[−1]≅−E 8⊕[+1]⊕[−1][+1]\oplus 9[-1] \cong -E_8\oplus[+1]\oplus[-1] (Sco. 05, p. 250-259) with the isomorphism coming from the Hesse-Minkowski theorem. It is therefore the same as for the topological 4-manifold M E 8¯#ℂP 2#*ℂP 2¯\overline{M_{E_8}}\#\mathbb{C}P^2\#*\overline{\mathbb{C}P^2} using the fake second complex projective space, so Freedman's classification claims both to be homeomorphic. In particular, that makes M E 8¯#ℂP 2#*ℂP 2¯\overline{M_{E_8}}\#\mathbb{C}P^2\#*\overline{\mathbb{C}P^2} smoothable. But a smooth surgery to remove ℂP 2\mathbb{C}P^2 results in the non-smoothable manifold M E 8¯#*ℂP 2¯\overline{M_{E_8}}\#*\overline{\mathbb{C}P^2}, (Sco. 05, p. 240-243 which follows from its intersection form −E 8⊕[−1]-E_8\oplus[-1] being negative definite and not diagonalizable, which Donaldson's theorem forbids if it was smoothable. The failure of this smooth surgery spawns an exotic R^4 in ℂP 2\mathbb{C}P^2.

Construction from a ten-fold blow-up

The previous construction can be expanded: The intersection form of the smooth complex surface ℂP 2#10ℂP 9¯\mathbb{C}P^2\# 10\overline{\mathbb{C}P^9} is [+1]⊕10[−1]≅−E 8⊕H⊕[−1][+1]\oplus 10[-1] \cong -E_8\oplus H\oplus[-1], (Sco. 05, p. 250-259) the same as for the topological 4-manifold M E 8¯#(S 2×S 2)#*ℂP 2¯\overline{M_{E_8}}\#(S^2\times S^2)\#*\overline{\mathbb{C}P^2}, so Freedman's classification claims both to be homeomorphic and in particular the latter to be smoothable. But a smooth surgery to remove S 2×S 2S^2\times S^2 again results in the non-smoothable manifold M E 8¯#*ℂP 2¯\overline{M_{E_8}}\#*\overline{\mathbb{C}P^2}. The failure of this smooth surgery spawns an exotic R^4 in S 2×S 2S^2\times S^2.

Construction from the failure of the smooth h-cobordism theorem

According to Michael Freedman, the four-dimensional topological h-cobordism theorem holds, while according to Simon Donaldson, the four-dimensional smooth h-cobordism theorem doesn’t hold. Let WW be a smooth h-cobordism between smooth 4-manifolds MM and NN, for which there is no diffeomorphism W≅M×[0,1]W\cong M\times[0,1], although there must be a homeomorphism W≅M×[0,1]W\cong M\times[0,1]. In this case there exists an open sub-cobordism U⊂WU\subset W with a homeomorphism U≅D 4×[0,1]≅ℝ 4×[0,1]U\cong D^4\times[0,1]\cong\mathbb{R}^4\times[0,1] and a compact contractible sub-cobordism K⊂UK\subset U, so that WW and UU are indeed smoothly trivial cobordisms outside of KK, hence there are diffeomorphisms:

W∖K≅(M∖K)×[0,1]; W\setminus K \cong(M\setminus K)\times[0,1];
U∖K≅(U∩M∖K)×[0,1]. U\setminus K \cong(U\cap M\setminus K)\times[0,1].

Now UU connects the two open subsets U∩M∖KU\cap M\setminus K and U∩N∖KU\cap N\setminus K, which are homeomorphic to ℝ 4\mathbb{R}^4, but cannot diffeomorphic to ℝ 4\mathbb{R}^4 as this would cause a diffeomorphism W≅M×[0,1]W\cong M\times[0,1]. (Sco. 05, p. 250-259)
