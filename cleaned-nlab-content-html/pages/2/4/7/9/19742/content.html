Idea

Among all special orthogonal groups SO(n)SO(n), the case of SO(8)SO(8) is special, since in the ADE classification of simple Lie groups it corresponds to D4, which makes its representation theory enjoy triality.

Octonionic construction of representations

The group Spin(8)Spin(8) has three 8-dimensional irreducible real representations: the right- and left-handed spinor representations S +S_+ and S −S_-, and the vector representation VV, which factors through SO(8)SO(8). (Bryant 20) gives a unified construction of all three representations using octonions, as follows.

For x∈𝕆x \in \mathbb{O} define a linear map m x:𝕆 2→𝕆 2m_x \colon \mathbb{O}^2 \to \mathbb{O}^2 as follows:

m x=(0 CR x −CL x 0) m_x = \left( \begin{array}{cc} 0 & C R_x \\ -C L_x & 0 \end{array} \right)

where L x,R x,C:𝕆→𝕆L_x, R_x, C \colon \mathbb{O} \to \mathbb{O} are left multiplication by xx, right multiplication by xx and octonionic conjugation, respectively. Since m x 2=−|x| 2m_x^2 = -{|x|}^2 this map induces an action of the Clifford algebra Cliff(𝕆)Cliff(\mathbb{O}) on 𝕆 2\mathbb{O}^2. (To be precise, this is Clifford algebra generated by 8 anticommuting square roots of minus 1). One can show that this action gives an isomorphism of real associative algebras

Cliff(𝕆)≅End(𝕆 2) Cliff(\mathbb{O}) \cong End(\mathbb{O}^2)

where End(𝕆 2)End(\mathbb{O}^2), the algebra of all real-linear transformations of 𝕆 2\mathbb{O}^2, is isomorphic to the algebra of 16×1616 \times 16 real matrices.

The group Spin(8)Spin(8) is isomorphic to the group of linear transformations generated by products m xm ym_x m_y where x,y∈𝕆x, y \in \mathbb{O} have |x|=|y|=1{|x|} = {|y|} = 1. This group is also generated by elements

(L u 0 0 R u) \left( \begin{array}{cc} L_u & 0 \\ 0 & R_u \end{array} \right)

where u∈𝕆u \in \mathbb{O} has |u|=1{|u|} = 1. In this sense we may say Spin(8)Spin(8) is generated by unit octonions uu, though octonion multiplication is not itself associative.

It follows that 𝕆 2\mathbb{O}^2 splits, as a representation of Spin(8)Spin(8), into two summands isomorphic to 𝕆\mathbb{O}. These are the two spinor representations of Spin(8)Spin(8). To match standard conventions Bryant calls the first summand, on which uu acts as L uL_u, the spinor representation S −S_-, and the second, on which uu acts as R uR_u, the spinor representation S +S_+. The vector representation of Spin(8)Spin(8) can also be identified with 𝕆\mathbb{O}, with uu acting as L uR uL_u R_u.

Bryant also describes the intertwiners V⊗S ±→S ∓V \otimes S_{\pm} \to S_{\mp} and the triality automorphisms of Spin(8)Spin(8) in these terms.
