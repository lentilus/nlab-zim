Idea

Principal U(1)U(1)-bundles (or principal SO(2)SO(2)-bundles) are principal bundles whose structure group is the unitary group U ( 1 ) U(1) (equivalently: the circle group, and special orthogonal group SO ( 2 ) SO(2) ).

Principal U(1)U(1)-bundles appear in multiple areas of mathematics, for example the Seiberg-Witten equations or monopole Floer homology. Since U(1)U(1) is the gauge group of electromagnetism, principal U(1)U(1)-bundles also appear promiently in theoretical physics (cf. fiber bundles in physics). For example, principal U(1)U(1)-bundles over the 2-sphere S 2S^2, which includes the complex Hopf fibration, can be used to describe the Dirac charge quantization of hypothetical three-dimensional (ℝ 3∖{0}≃S 2\mathbb{R}^3\setminus\{0\}\simeq S^2) magnetic monopoles, called Dirac monopoles, compare also with D=2 Yang-Mills theory.

Properties

Classification

Principal U(1)U(1)-bundles are classified by the classifying space BU(1) of the first unitary group U(1)U(1), which is the infinite complex projective space ℂP ∞\mathbb{C}P^\infty. (EU(1)≅ESO(2)E U(1)\cong E SO(2) is then the infinite-dimensional sphere S ∞S^\infty.) For a CW complex XX, one has a bijection:

[X,BU(1)]≅[X,ℂP ∞] →≅ Prin U(1)(X) [f] ↦ f *EU(1)≅f *S ∞ \begin{array}{ccc} [X,BU(1)] \cong [X,\mathbb{C}P^\infty] &\xrightarrow{\cong}& Prin_{U(1)}(X) \\ [f] &\mapsto& f^*E U(1) \cong f^*S^\infty \end{array}

(Mitchell 2011, Theorem 7.4).

Since U(1)U(1) is the Eilenberg-MacLane space K(ℤ,1)K(\mathbb{Z},1), one has that BU(1)B U(1) is K(ℤ,2)K(\mathbb{Z},2). (Hatcher 2001, Example 4.50.). Due to this, one can consider the identity c 1:BU(1)→K(ℤ,2)c_1\colon B U(1)\rightarrow K(\mathbb{Z},2), which is exactly the first Chern class and an isomorphism (Hatcher 2017, Theorem 3.10.). Postcomposition then creates a bijection to singular cohomology:

c 1:Prin U(1)(X)≅[X,BU(1)]→≅[X,K(ℤ,2)]≅H 2(X,ℤ). c_1 \colon Prin_{U(1)}(X) \cong [X,BU(1)] \xrightarrow{\cong} [X,K(\mathbb{Z},2)] \cong H^2(X,\mathbb{Z}) \mathrlap{\,.}

The infinite complex projective space ℂP ∞\mathbb{C}P^\infty is a CW complex, whose nn-skeleton is ℂP k\mathbb{C}P^k with the largest natural k∈ℕk\in\mathbb{N} fulfilling 2k≤n2k\leq n. For an nn-dimensional CW complex XX, the cellular approximation theorem (Hatcher 2001, Theorem 4.8.) states that every map X→ℍP ∞X \rightarrow \mathbb{H}P^\infty is homotopic to a cellular map, which in particular factorizes over the canonical inclusion ℂP k↪ℂP ∞\mathbb{C}P^k\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}P^\infty. As a result, the postcomposition [X,ℂP k]→[X,ℂP ∞][X,\mathbb{C}P^k]\rightarrow[X,\mathbb{C}P^\infty] is surjective. In particular for XX having no more than three dimension, one has k=1k=1 with ℂP 1≅S 2\mathbb{C}P^1\cong S^2. Hence there is a connection to cohomotopy:

π 2(X)→Prin U(1)(X) \pi^2(X) \rightarrow Prin_{U(1)}(X)

Its composition with the first Chern class is exactly the Hurewicz homomorphism π 2(X)→H 2(X,ℤ)\pi^2(X)\rightarrow H^2(X,\mathbb{Z}).

Associated vector bundle

For principal U(1)U(1)-bundles P↠XP\twoheadrightarrow X, there is an associated complex line bundle L=P× U(1)ℂ↠XL=P\times_{U(1)}\mathbb{C}\twoheadrightarrow X using the balanced product. If QQ is the induced principal SU(2)-bundle (using the canonical inclusion U(1)↪SU(2),z↦diag(z,z −1)U(1)\hookrightarrow SU(2),z\mapsto diag(z,z^{-1})), then its adjoint bundle is given by:

Ad(Q)=L 2⊕ℝ̲. Ad(Q) =L^2\oplus\underline{\mathbb{R}}.

(Donaldson & Kronheimer 91, Eq. (4.2.12))

Examples

  • The canonical projection S 2n+1↠ℂP nS^{2n+1}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{C}P^n is a principal U(1)U(1)-bundle. For n=1n=1 using ℂP 1≅S 2\mathbb{C}P^1\cong S^2, the complex Hopf fibration S 3↠S 2S^3\twoheadrightarrow S^2 is a special case. In the general case, the classifying map is given by the canonical inclusion:

    ℂP n↪ℂP ∞≅BU(1). \mathbb{C}P^n \hookrightarrow \mathbb{C}P^\infty \cong B U(1).

  • One has S 2n+1≅U(n+1)/U(n)S^{2n+1}\cong U(n+1)/U(n), hence there is a principal U(1)-bundle S 3↠U(2)S^3\twoheadrightarrow U(2). Such principal bundles are classified by:

    π 3BU(1)≅π 2U(1)≅1. \pi_3 B U(1) \cong \pi_2 U(1) \cong 1\mathrlap{\,.}

    (Mitchell 2011, Corollary 11.2.)

Hence the principal bundle is trivial, which fits SU(2)≅S 3SU(2)\cong S^3 and U(n)≅SU(n)×U(1)U(n)\cong\SU(n)\times U(1). * One has S n≅SO(n+1)/SO(n)S^n\cong SO(n+1)/SO(n), hence there is a principal SO(2)-bundle SO(3)↠S 2SO(3)\twoheadrightarrow S^2. Such principal bundles are classified by:

π 2BU(1)≅π 1U(1)≅π 1S 1≅ℤ. \pi_2 B U(1) \cong \pi_1 U(1) \cong \pi_1 S^1 \cong \mathbb{Z}.

(Mitchell 2011, Corollary 11.2.)
