Idea

In the context of real-oriented cohomology theory, notably in KR-theory, by “real space” (“real manifold”) one means a topological space (a manifold) equipped with an action of the cyclic group of order 2 ℤ/2\mathbb{Z}/2 (a “non-linear real structure”).

In the context of string theory real spaces appear as orientifold target spacetimes. The involution fixed points here are known as O-planes.

Examples

Real circles

There are three non-equivalent real structures on the circle S 1S^1, usually denoted

  • S 2,0S^{2,0} for the trivial involution;

  • S 1,1S^{1,1} for the reflection involution (identifying the two semi-circles);

  • S 0,2S^{0,2} for the antipodal involution (rotation by π\pi).

Accordingly real-oriented cohomology theory is bigraded in a way modeled on this bigrading.

(This is standard notation, but maybe S 1,0S^{1,0}, S 12,12,S 0,1S^{\tfrac{1}{2}, \tfrac{1}{2}}, S^{0,1} would be more suggestive. Indeed the quotients in the first and the last case are actually circles, while in the second case it is the semi-circle.)

Complexified cartesian spaces

The complex nn-dimensional complexified cartesian space ℂ n{\mathbb{C}}^n equipped with its conjugative involution is a real space. Explicitly, this involution sends (z 1,…,z n)(z^1,\ldots, z^n) to (z 1¯,…,z n¯)(\bar{z^1},\ldots, \bar{z^n}).

Complex projective spaces.

The complex nn-dimensional complex projective space ℙ ℂ n{\mathbb{P}}^n_{\mathbb{C}} equipped with a conjugation involution is a real space. For each choice of affine chart, the conjugation involution of this chart (which is biholomorphic to ℂ n{\mathbb{C}}^n) extends to a conjugation involution on ℙ ℂ n{\mathbb{P}}^n_{\mathbb{C}}. Any two conjugation involutions are ℤ/2{\mathbb{Z}}/2-equivariantly diffeomorphic.

For example, the Riemann surface ℙ ℂ 1{\mathbb{P}}^1_{\mathbb{C}} is diffeomorphic to the 2-sphere S 2S^2 and its conjugation involution is the antipodal action.

Complex general and special linear groups.

For AA a square matrix, the determinant of its conjugation transpose equals the conjugate of its determinant. In symbols, detA *=detA¯{\mathrm{det}} A^* = \overline{{\mathrm{det}} A}. Hence sending a square matrix to its conjugate transpose is an involution on the complex general GL(n,ℂ)GL(n,{\mathbb{C}}) and special SL(n,ℂ)SL(n,{\mathbb{C}}).

In particular, the real space SL(1,ℂ)SL(1,{\mathbb{C}}) equipped with this conjugate transpose involution is equivariantly diffeomorphic to S 0,2S^{0,2}, the circle equipped with its antipodal action (dicussed above).

Properties

Relation to real cobordism

There is a “real” analog of complex cobordism cohomology theory MUMU, the MR cohomology theory MℝM \mathbb{R}.

While π •(Mℝ)\pi_\bullet(M \mathbb{R}) is not the cobordism ring of real manifolds, still every real manifold does give a class in MℝM \mathbb{R} (Kriz 01, p. 13). For details see here: pdf.
