Motivation

The original motivation for the notion of separable functors [Năstăsescu, van den Bergh & van Oystaeyen (1989)] was the functorial form of Maschke's theorem over rings and its various cousins: namely the classical Maschke’s theorem about finite group rings over fields, generalizes to the statements that when the order of the group is invertible in the ground ring then the splitting of an exact sequence of k G k G -modules can be obtained functorially from its splitting as an exact sequence of underlying k k -modules.

This “Maschke property” of the forgetful functor kGMod→ kMod{}_{k G} Mod\to {}_{k}Mod is what is generalized by the notion of separable functors.

A further example appears in the study of ring extensions f:R→Sf \colon R\to S: Such is separable iff the corresponding restriction of scalars-functor Res R S: SMod→ RMod\operatorname{Res}_R^S \colon {}_{S}Mod \to {}_{R}Mod is a separable functor.

Definition

Let F:C→DF \colon C\to D be a functor with hom-set components

F x,y: C(x,y) ⟶ D(Fx,Fy) (x→fy) ↦ (Fx→FfFy). \array{ F_{x,y} \colon & C(x,y)&\longrightarrow& D(F x,F y) \\ & (x\stackrel{f}\to y)&\mapsto& (F x\stackrel{F f}\to F y) \mathrlap{\,.} }

Then FF is called separable [Năstăsescu, van den Bergh & van Oystaeyen (1989), p. 398] if each F x,yF_{x,y} has a section which is “natural” in xx and yy, in a suitable sense.

Properties

Theorem

(Rafael’s theorem) Let F⊣GF\dashv G be a pair of adjoint functors. Then

  • FF is separable iff the adjunction unit η:id→GF\eta \colon id \to G F has a section (= a natural transformation ν\nu which is its right inverse, η∘ν=1\eta\circ\nu = 1),

  • GG is separable iff the adjunction counit ϵ:FG→id\epsilon \colon F G \to id has a retraction (i.e. a natural transformation ζ\zeta that is its left inverse, ζ∘ϵ=1\zeta\circ\epsilon =1).

The latter condition is reminiscent of one of the many equivalent definitions of a separable algebra AA over a field, namely one for which multiplication, viewed as an (A,A)(A,A)-bimodule map A⊗A op→AA \otimes A^{\mathrm{op}} \to A, has a right inverse.
