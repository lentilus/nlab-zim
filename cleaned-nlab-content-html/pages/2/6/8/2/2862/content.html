gebra theory

Idea

Gebras denote algebras, coalgebras and their combinations. They generalize algebras over operads and similar notions by possibly involving cooperations. Common frameworks include properads.

In modern algebra, dealing with co-operations A→A ⊗nA\to A^{\otimes n} instead of only with operations A ⊗n→AA^{\otimes n}\to A for some monoidal product ⊗\otimes, is a specialty of a group of people who are often centered around the study of Hopf algebras, but the subject is much wider, including internal co(al)gebras, cogroups in cartesian categories, Tannaka reconstruction, PROPs (which have even operations taking mm arguments to kk arguments, hence generalizing both operations and co-operations), co-operads, bi(al)gebroids, Hopf algebroids, entwining structures, mixed distributive laws and so on. Common tools are graphical calculations (extending also to planar algebras and alike), Sweedler notation and so on.

Historical note

We may call this area the theory of gebras as in the article Serre 1993.

Bourbaki complained that in Arabic ‘al’ is an article so classical ‘bi-’ for two is incompatible with it. Thus Bourbaki and much of the French school makes it a point to say ‘cogebra, bigebra’ instead of ‘coalgebra, bialgebra’.
