on the real projective space in homotopy type theory.

Abstract. Homotopy type theory is a version of Martin-Löf type theory taking advantage of its homotopical models. In particular, we can use and construct objects of homotopy theory and reason about them using higher inductive types. In this article, we construct the real projective spaces, key players in homotopy theory, as certain higher inductive types in homotopy type theory. The classical definition of ℝ P n \mathbb{R}P^n , as the quotient space identifying antipodal points of the n n -sphere, does not translate directly to homotopy type theory. Instead, we define ℝP n\mathbb{R}P^n by induction on nn simultaneously with its tautological bundle of 22-element sets. As the base case, we take ℝP −1\mathbb{R}P^{-1} to be the empty type. In the inductive step, we take ℝP n+1\mathbb{R}P^{n+1} to be the mapping cone of the projection map of the tautological bundle of ℝP n\mathbb{R}P^{n}, and we use its universal property and the univalence axiom to define the tautological bundle on ℝP n+1\mathbb{R}P^{n+1}. By showing that the total space of the tautological bundle of ℝP n\mathbb{R}P^n is the n-sphere, we retrieve the classical description of ℝP n+1\mathbb{R}P^{n+1} as ℝP(n)\mathbb{R}P(n) with an (n+1)(n+1)-cell attached to it. The infinite dimensional real projective space, defined as the sequential colimit of the ℝP n\mathbb{R}P^n with the canonical inclusion maps, is equivalent to the Eilenberg-MacLane space K(ℤ/2ℤ,1)K(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},1), which here arises as the subtype of the universe consisting of 22-element types. Indeed, the infinite dimensional projective space classifies the 00-sphere bundles, which one can think of as synthetic line bundles. These constructions in homotopy type theory further illustrate the utility of homotopy type theory, including the interplay of type theoretic and homotopy theoretic ideas.
