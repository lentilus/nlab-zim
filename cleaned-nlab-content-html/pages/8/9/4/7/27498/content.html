Idea

Taketa’s theorem and related statements say that a necessary condition on a finite group to have all its complex irreps be suitably induced representations is that it is a solvable group.

Terminology: “M-groups”

A finite group GG is called an MM-group if all its complex irreducible representations are induced from 1-dimensional representations, hence if for each irrep rhorho of GG there exists a subgroup H⊂GH \subset G and a 1-dimensional rep ν\nu of HH, such that ρ≃Ind H Gρ\rho \,\simeq\, Ind^G_H \rho, where Ind H G:HRep→GRepInd_H^G \,\colon\, H Rep \to G Rep is the induction functor.

More generally, for k∈ℕ +k \in \mathbb{N}_+ a positive natural number, a finite group is called an M kM_k-group if all its irreps are induced from irreps of dimension ≤k\leq k. (cf. Berkovich 1996)

Statement

Theorem

(Taketa’s theorem)
M-groups are solvable.

(Taketa 1930, Isaacs 1976 thm. 5.12)

In fact:

Theorem

If a finite group has the property that for each of its complex irreps of dimension ≥2\geq 2 some multiple of it is induced from an irrep of a proper subgroup, then the group is solvable.

(LMT 2013)
