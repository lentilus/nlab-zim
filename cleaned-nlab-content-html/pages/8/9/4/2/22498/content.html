Idea

The representation theory of the symmetric groups.

Properties

Irreducible representations

Proposition

In characteristic zero, the irreducible representations of the symmetric group are, up to isomorphism, given by the Specht modules labeled by partitions λ∈Part(n)\lambda \in Part(n).

(e.g. Sagan 01, Thm. 2.4.6).

Dimension of irreps and hook length

Over the complex numbers:

The dimension of the irrep S (λ)S^{(\lambda)} (Specht module) equals the number of standard Young tableau of shape λ\lambda:

(1)dim(S (λ))=|sYTableaux λ| dim\big(S^{(\lambda)}\big) \;=\; \left\vert sYTableaux_\lambda \right\vert

(e.g. Sagan, Thm. 2.6.5)

Moreover, the number of standard Young tableaux of shape λ\lambda is given by the hook length formula

(2)|sYTableaux λ|=n!(∏ 1≤i≤rows(λ)1≤j≤λ iℓhook λ(i,j)) −1. \left\vert sYTableaux_\lambda \right\vert \;=\; n! \, \left( \prod_{ { 1 \leq i \leq rows(\lambda) } \atop { 1 \leq j \leq \lambda_i } } \ell hook_\lambda(i,j) \right)^{-1} \,.

This is due to Frame, Robinson & Thrall 54. Textbook accounts include Stanley 99, Cor. 7.21.6, Sagan 01 Thm. 3.10.2.

Combining (1) with (2) gives the hook length formula for the dimension of the Specht modules

dim(S (λ))=n!(∏ 1≤i≤rows(λ)1≤j≤λ iℓhook λ(i,j)) −1. dim\big(S^{(\lambda)}\big) \;=\; n! \left( \prod_{ { 1 \leq i \leq rows(\lambda) } \atop { 1 \leq j \leq \lambda_i } } \ell hook_\lambda(i,j) \right)^{-1} \,.

(e.g. James 78, Thm. 20.1)

Examples

The standard representation

Example

(standard representation)
What is sometimes called the standard representation of the symmetric group Sym nSym_n is the quotient of the defining nn-dimensional permutation representation of Sym nSym_n on k nk^n (via permutation of the canonical basis vectors) by the 1d trivial representation inside that, hence the (n−1)(n-1)-dimensional subspace where the sum of coefficients of the canonical basis vectors is zero.

This is an irreducible representation whose corresponding partition (according to Prop. ) is ((n−1),1)\big((n-1),1\big).

(cf. Fulton & Harris 1991 p 9 & Ex. 4.6, Kao 2010 Def. 2.5, Groupprops: Standard representation of the symmetric group)

Example

The unitarization of the standard representation of the symmetric group Sym 3Sym_3 has two generating permutations represented by (what in quantum information theory is called)

  1. the Pauli Z-gate ZZ,

  2. the rotation gate R y(8π/3)=−R y(2π/3)R_y(8\pi/3) = -R_y(2\pi/3).

Proof

The defining permutation representation of Sym 3Sym_3 — with canonical linear basis {e 1,e 2,e 3}\{e_1, e_2, e_3\} acted on as σe i=e σ(i) \sigma e_i \,=\, e_{\sigma(i)} — is unitary with respect to the canonical inner product ⟨e i,e j⟩≡δ ij\langle e_i, e_j\rangle \equiv \delta_{i j}.

Therefore a unitary form of the standard representation is obtained as the orthogonal complement of the trivial subrepresentation spanned by e 1+e 2+e 3e_1 + e_2 + e_3.

One orthonormal linear basis for this orthogonal complement is clearly:

([1 0]≔16(e 1+e 2−2e 3),[0 1]≔12(e 1−e 2)). \Bigg( \; \left[ \begin{array}{c} 1 \\ 0 \end{array} \right] \;\coloneqq\; \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{6}} (e_1 + e_2 - 2 e_3) \;,\,\;\;\; \left[ \begin{array}{c} 0 \\ 1 \end{array} \right] \;\coloneqq\; \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} (e_1 - e_2) \; \Bigg) \,.

On this basis, the transposition (213)(213) acts as

(213)16(e 1+e 2−2e 3)=16(e 1+e 2−2e 3), (213) \, \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{6}} (e_1 + e_2 - 2 e_3) \;=\; \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{6}} (e_1 + e_2 - 2 e_3) \,,

and

(213)12(e 1−e 2)=−12(e 1−e 2), (213) \, \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} (e_1 - e_2) \;=\; - \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} (e_1 - e_2) \,,

hence is given by the matrix

[213]≡[1 0 0 −1]⏟Z, [213] \;\equiv\; \underset{ Z }{ \underbrace{ \left[ \begin{array}{c} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{array} \right] } } \,,

which in quantum information theory is called the Pauli Z-gate.

On the other hand, the cyclic permutation (231)(231) acts as

(231)16(e 1+e 2−2e 3)=16(−2e 1+e 2+e 3)=−1216(e 1+e 2−2e 3)−3212(e 1−e 2) (231) \, \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{6}}(e_1 + e_2 - 2 e_3) \;=\; \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{6}}(- 2 e_1 + e_2 + e_3) \;=\; -\tfrac{1}{2} \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{6}} (e_1 + e_2 - 2 e_3) - \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(e_1 - e_2)

and

(231)12(e 1−e 2)=12(e 2−e 3)=3216(e 1+e 2−2e 3)−1212(e 1−e 2) (231) \, \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(e_1 - e_2) \;=\; \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(e_2 - e_3) \;=\; \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{6}}(e_1 + e_2 - 2 e_3) - \tfrac{1}{2} \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} (e_1 - e_2)

and is hence given by the matrix

[231]=−[1/2 −3/2 3/2 1/2]=[cos(4π/3) −sin(4π/3) sin(4π/3) cos(4π/3)]⏟R y(4π23), [231] \;=\; - \left[ \begin{array}{cc} 1/2 & - \sqrt{3}/2 \\ \sqrt{3}/2 & 1/2 \end{array} \right] \;=\; \underset{ R_y(4\pi \tfrac{2}{3}) }{ \underbrace{ \left[ \begin{array}{cc} cos(4\pi/3) & - sin(4\pi/3) \\ sin(4\pi/3) & cos(4\pi/3) \end{array} \right] } } \,,

known in quantum information theory as a rotation gate.
