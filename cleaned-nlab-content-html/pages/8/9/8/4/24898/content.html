Idea

A geometric hyperdoctrine is a hyperdoctrine with respect to lattices that are frames.

 Definition

Let CC be a category with finite limits. A geometric hyperdoctrine over CC is a functor

P:C op→Frm P : C^{op} \to Frm

from the opposite category of CC to the category of frames, such that for every morphism f:A→Bf : A \to B in CC, the functor P(A)→P(B)P(A) \to P(B) has a left adjoint ∃ f\exists_f satisfying

  1. Frobenius reciprocity;

  2. Beck-Chevalley condition.

Examples

Let OLocOLoc be the category of overt locales, and let FrmFrm be the category of frames. The functor 𝒪:OLoc op→Frm\mathcal{O}:OLoc^\op \to Frm that takes an overt locale to its frame of opens is a geometric hyperdoctrine.

Let SetSet be the category of sets, defined as a Grothendieck topos on the singleton, and let FrmFrm be the category of frames. The subobject poset functor Sub:Set op→Frm\mathrm{Sub}:Set^\op \to Frm that takes a set to its subobject poset is a geometric hyperdoctrine.

This could be generalized to any geometric category CC: the subobject poset functor Sub:C op→Frm\mathrm{Sub}:C^\op \to Frm that takes an object of CC to its subobject poset is a geometric hyperdoctrine.
