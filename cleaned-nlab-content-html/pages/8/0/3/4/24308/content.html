Idea

In type theory, the family of finite types is the analog of the natural numbers-indexed set of finite sets in set theory.

Definition

In type theory, given a natural number n:ℕn \colon\mathbb{N}, the family of finite types Fin 𝒰(n)\mathrm{Fin}_\mathcal{U}(n) of a type universe 𝒰\mathcal{U}

  • with empty type ∅ 𝒰:𝒰\varnothing_\mathcal{U} \colon \mathcal{U},

  • unit type * 𝒰:𝒰\ast_\mathcal{U}:\mathcal{U},

  • and binary coproducts (−)⊔ 𝒰(−):𝒰×𝒰→𝒰(-) \sqcup_\mathcal{U}(-) \colon \mathcal{U} \times \mathcal{U} \to \mathcal{U}

is an inductive family, inductively defined by:

Fin 𝒰(0) ≔ ∑A:𝒰(A≅ 𝒰∅ 𝒰) Fin 𝒰(s(n)) ≔ ∑A:𝒰[∑ B:Fin 𝒰(n)(A≅ 𝒰(B⊔ 𝒰* 𝒰))] \begin{array}{lcl} \mathrm{Fin}_\mathcal{U}(0) &\coloneqq& \underset {A \colon \mathcal{U}} {\sum} \big( A \cong_\mathcal{U} \varnothing_\mathcal{U} \big) \\ \mathrm{Fin}_\mathcal{U}(s(n)) &\coloneqq& \underset {A:\mathcal{U}} {\sum} \bigg[ \sum_{B:\mathrm{Fin}_\mathcal{U}(n)} \Big( A \cong_\mathcal{U} \big( B \sqcup_\mathcal{U} \ast_\mathcal{U} \big) \Big) \bigg] \end{array}

(where “∑\sum” denotes dependent sum and “≅\cong” denotes identity types, as usual).
