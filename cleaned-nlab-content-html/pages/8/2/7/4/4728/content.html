Idea

A surface is a space of dimension 2, usually understood to be connected.

In differential topology/differential geometry this means a 2-dimensional (differentiable/smooth) manifold.

In complex analytic geometry this usually means a complex manifold of complex dimension 2 (hence real dimension 4).

Similarly and more generally, in algebraic geometry an algebraic surface is a variety of algebraic dimension 2.

Properties

Classification

The oriented closed real surfaces are classified, up to isomorphism, by a natural number g∈ℕg \in \mathbb{N} called the genus (intuitively its “number of holes”, in a sense):

genusoriented surface
002-sphere S 2S^2
112-torus 𝕋 2\mathbb{T}^2
22double torus Σ 2 2\Sigma^2_2

(cf. Kinsey 1991 Thm. 4.14, Gallier & Xu 2013 Thm 6.3, Bray et al. Thm. 4.14)

Here the closed oriented surface Σ g 2\Sigma^2_g of genus gg may be obtained from the regular 4 g 4g -gon by identifying (cf. Giblin 1977 Thm 2.8):

  • all the boundary vertices with a single point,

and, going clockwise for k∈{0,⋯,g−1}k \in \{0, \cdots, g-1\},

  • the 4k+14k+1st boundary edge with the reverse of the 4k+34k +3rd,

  • the 4k+24k+2nd edge with the reverse of the 4k+44k+4th.

(This and the following graphics are taken from SS25.)

Similarly, the non-orientable closed real surfaces are classified by a positive natural number h∈ℕ ≥1h \in \mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}, also called the (non-orientable) genus or the number of crosscaps

# crosscapsnon-orientable surface
11projective plane ℝP 2\mathbb{R}P^2
22Klein bottle ℝP 2#ℝP 2\mathbb{R}P^2 # \mathbb{R}P^2

Here the non-orientable surface Σ g¯ 2\Sigma^2_{\overline{g}} with hh crosscaps may be obtained from the regular 2 h 2h -gon by identifying

  • all the boundary vertices with a single point,

and, going clockwise for k∈{0,⋯,h−1}k \in \{0, \cdots, h-1\},

  • the 2k+12k+1st boundary edge with the 2k+22k + 2nd.

(This is a conveniently concise but not the most intuitively visualized choice of fundamental polygons – other choices are possible and often discussed, cf. also MO:q/172784.)

CoHomology

Proposition

The ordinary homology with integer coefficients of a closed oriented surface Σ g\Sigma_g of genus GG is the free abelian group on 2g2g generators:

H 1(Σ g;ℤ)≃ℤ 2g. H_1\big( \Sigma_g ;\, \mathbb{Z} \big) \;\simeq\; \mathbb{Z}^{2g} \,.

(e.g. Giblin 1977 Thm 6.13)

By the universal coefficient theorem the analogous result holds for the integral cohomology

H 1(Σ g;ℤ)≃ℤ 2g. H^1\big( \Sigma_g ;\, \mathbb{Z} \big) \;\simeq\; \mathbb{Z}^{2g} \,.

It follows for instance that:

Proposition

On a closed oriented surface of genus g∈ℕg \in \mathbb{N} there exist precisely 2 2g2^{2g} distinct spin structures.

Proof

Since spin structures are classified by H 1(−;C 2)H_1(-;C_2) (see there) this follows by Prop. and the universal coefficient theorem.

Cf. Atiyah 1971, Lawson & Michelson 1989 Ex. 2.6.
