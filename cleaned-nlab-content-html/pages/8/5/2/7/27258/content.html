Z-functor

Definition

A ℤ\mathbb{Z}-functor or integers functor is a functor from the category of commutative rings CRing to the category of sets Set. The category of ℤ\mathbb{Z}-functors is thus the functor category Set CRing\mathrm{Set}^\mathrm{CRing}.

Examples

  • The forgetful functor 𝔸 1:CRing→Set\mathbb{A}^1:\mathrm{CRing} \to \mathrm{Set} is a ℤ\mathbb{Z}-functor.

  • An affine scheme is a representable ℤ\mathbb{Z}-functor.

Properties

Definition

Let XX be a ℤ\mathbb{Z}-functor. Then the ring of fractions 𝒪(X)\mathcal{O}(X) of XX is the set X⇒𝔸 1X \Rightarrow \mathbb{A}^1 of natural transformations from XX to the forgetful functor 𝔸 1\mathbb{A}^1.

The ring structure on X⇒𝔸 1X \Rightarrow \mathbb{A}^1 is defined pointwise by the following, for every commutative ring RR and element x∈X(R)x \in X(R):

0 R(x)≔01 R(x)≔10_R(x) \coloneqq 0 \qquad 1_R(x) \coloneqq 1
(a R+b R)(x)≔a R(x)+b R(x)(a_R + b_R)(x) \coloneqq a_R(x) + b_R(x)
(−a R)(x)≔−a R(x)(- a_R)(x) \coloneqq - a_R(x)
(a R⋅b R)(x)≔a R(x)⋅b R(x)(a_R \cdot b_R)(x) \coloneqq a_R(x) \cdot b_R(x)
