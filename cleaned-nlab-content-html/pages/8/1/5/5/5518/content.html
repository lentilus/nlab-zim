Idea

Gerbes give a nice way to group together bundle data on a smooth manifold, but gerbes also naturally define degree two cohomology. Thus the idea of using gerbes in differential geometry is to have a nice language that relates geometric concepts such as connections and curvature to cohomological classifications.

In addition, one can use the analogies above that are made precise with gerbes to define other new concepts such as 3-curvature and “local” fiberwise connections.

Definitions

  • Let XX be a smooth manifold. A Dixmier-Douady sheaf of groupoids over XX is a ℂ̲ X *\underline{\mathbb{C}}_X^*- gerbe on XX where ℂ̲ X *\underline{\mathbb{C}}_X^* is the sheaf of smooth ℂ *\mathbb{C}^*-valued functions (not to be confused with the constant sheaf ℂ X *\mathbb{C}_X^*).

  • We define ℤ(1)\mathbb{Z}(1) to be the term in the exponential sequence on XX: 0→ℤ(1)→ℂ̲ X→ℂ̲ X *→00\to \mathbb{Z}(1)\to \underline{\mathbb{C}}_X \to \underline{\mathbb{C}}_X^*\to 0.

Preliminaries

Taking the associated sequence in cohomology to the exponential sequence gives us an isomorphism H 2(X,ℂ̲ X *)→∼H 3(X,ℤ(1))H^2(X, \underline{\mathbb{C}}_X^*)\overset\sim\to H^3(X, \mathbb{Z}(1)).

We have a canonical isomorphism between the group of equivalence classes of Dixmier-Douady sheaves of groupoids over XX (basically by the definition of 𝒜\mathcal{A}-gerbe) and H 2(X,ℂ̲ X *)≃H 3(X,ℤ(1))H^2(X, \underline{\mathbb{C}}_X^*)\simeq H^3(X, \mathbb{Z}(1)).

Geometric Interpretation of H 3(X,ℤ(1))H^3(X, \mathbb{Z}(1))

Idea: Classes in H 3(X,ℤ(1))H^3(X, \mathbb{Z}(1)) correspond to principal GG- bundles over XX where GG is the projective linear group of a separable Hilbert space, namely C ∞(𝕋)C^\infty (\mathbb{T}).
