Definition

An algebraic number is a root of a non-zero polynomial with integer coefficients (or, equivalently, with rational coefficients). Equivalently, an element α\alpha of a field extension KK of the rational numbers ℚ\mathbb{Q} is algebraic if the subfield ℚ(α)\mathbb{Q}(\alpha) is a finite degree extension, i.e., is finite-dimensional as a rational vector space.

Since the rational numbers are a subfield of the complex numbers, and since the complex numbers are an algebraically closed field, algebraic numbers are naturally regarded as a sub-field of complex numbers

ℚ↪ℂ. \mathbb{Q} \hookrightarrow \mathbb{C} \,.

But the collection of all algebraic numbers forms itself already an algebraically closed field, typically denoted ℚ¯\overline{\mathbb{Q}}, as this is the algebraic closure of the field ℚ\mathbb{Q} of rational numbers. This also follows easily from the equivalent definition of algebraic numbers in terms of finite degree extensions. The absolute Galois group Gal(ℚ¯,ℚ)Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}, \mathbb{Q}) is peculiar, see there.

Given a field kk, an algebraic number field KK over kk is a finite-degree extension of kk. By default, the term “algebraic number field” means an algebraic number field over the rational numbers. If α\alpha is an algebraic number over ℚ\mathbb{Q} then ℚ[α]\mathbb{Q}[\alpha] is a number field, however the field of all algebraic numbers is not a number field.

An algebraic integer is a root of a monic polynomial with integer coefficients. Equivalently, an element α\alpha of a field extension KK of ℚ\mathbb{Q} is an algebraic integer if the ring ℤ[α]\mathbb{Z}[\alpha] is of finite rank as a ℤ\mathbb{Z}-module. It follows easily from this characterization that the collection of all algebraic integers forms a commutative ring.
