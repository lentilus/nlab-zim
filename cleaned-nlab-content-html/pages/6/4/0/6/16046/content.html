Idea

What is known as the prime geodesic theorem is a result that descrives the asymptotic distribution of prime geodesics on a hyperbolic manifold.

The prime geodesic theorem is analogous to the prime number theorem. Just as the zeros of the Riemann zeta function control the error term in the prime number theorem, so the zeros of the Selberg zeta function control the error term in the prime geodesic theorem.

Statement

Let XX be a hyperbolic manifold of dimension d+1d + 1. Let

Γ≔π 1(X) \Gamma \coloneqq \pi_1(X)

be its fundamental group.

For each element γ∈Γ\gamma \in \Gamma there exists one closed geodesic in XX representing it. By standard convenient abuse of notation we write γ\gamma also for that geodesic. Write

  • l(γ)l(\gamma) for the length of the geodesic γ\gamma;

  • N(γ)≔e l(γ)N(\gamma) \coloneqq e^{l(\gamma)} for what is called its norm;

  • π Γ(x)\pi_\Gamma(x) for the number of primitive elements γ∈Γ\gamma \in \Gamma such that N(γ)≤xN(\gamma) \leq x.

The prime geodesic theorem is a statement of the form that this number π Γ(x)\pi_\Gamma(x) satisfies

π Γ(x)=li(x d)+∑n=0Nli(x s n)+(errorterm) \pi_\Gamma(x) = li(x^d) + \underoverset{n = 0}{N}{\sum} li(x^{s_n}) + (error\;term)

where

  • li(x)=∫0xdtlntli(x) = \underoverset{0}{x}{\int}\frac{d t}{ln t} is the logarithmic integral function;

  • {s 1,⋯,s N}\{s_1, \cdots, s_N\} are the zeros of the Selberg zeta function in the interval (d/2,d)(d/2,d)

  • (errorterm)(error\;term) is some term, estimates of which are the main content of this class of theorems.
