Definition

Consider a real-valued smooth function

(1)f:M⟶ℝ f \colon M \longrightarrow \mathbb{R}

on a smooth manifold MM.

Definition

A point p∈Mp\in M is a critical point of ff (1) if for any smooth curve γ:(−ϵ,ϵ)→M\gamma : (-\epsilon, \epsilon)\to M with γ(0)=p\gamma(0)=p, the tangent vector

d(f∘γ)dt| t=0=0. \frac{d(f\circ\gamma)}{dt} |_{t=0} = 0 \,.

The critical point is regular if for one (or equivalently any) chart ϕ:U open→ℝ n\phi : U^{\open}\to \mathbb{R}^n, where p∈Up\in U and ϕ(p)=0∈ℝ n\phi(p) = 0\in \mathbb{R}^n, the Hessian matrix

(∂ 2(f∘ϕ −1)∂x i∂x j(0)) i,j=1,…,n\left(\frac{\partial^2 (f\circ \phi^{-1})}{\partial x^i\partial x^j}(0)\right)_{i,j=1,\ldots, n}

is a nondegenerate (i.e. maximal rank) matrix.

Definition

The function ff is called a Morse function if every critical point of ff is regular (Def. ).

A choice of a Morse function on a compact manifold is often used to study topology of the manifold. This is called the Morse theory.

Properties

One of the basic tools of Morse theory is the Morse lemma.
