Statement

The following is known as the Gauss multiplication formula or the Legendre relation:

Proposition

For

  • a positive integer N∈ℕ +N \in \mathbb{N}_+,

  • any z∈ℝ∖{0,−1/N,−2/N,⋯}z \in \mathbb{R} \setminus \{0, -1/N, -2/N, \cdots\} ,

the Gamma function Γ(−)\Gamma(-) satisfies

∏j=0N−1Γ(z+jN)=(2π) 12(N−1)⋅N (12−Nz)⋅Γ(Nz). \underoverset {j = 0} {N-1} {\prod} \Gamma \left( z + \tfrac{j}{N} \right) \;=\; (2 \pi)^{ \tfrac{1}{2}(N-1) } \cdot N^{ (\tfrac{1}{2} - N z) } \cdot \Gamma( N z ) \,.

Example

In the special case of N=2N = 2 this is known as the duplication formula:

Γ(z)⋅Γ(z+1/2)=(2π) 1/2⋅2 1/2−2z⋅Γ(2z). \Gamma(z) \cdot \Gamma \big( z + 1/2 \big) \;=\; (2\pi)^{1/2} \cdot 2^{1/2 - 2z} \cdot \Gamma(2 z) \,.

Remark

A related expression gives a product of Barnes G-functions (see there):

∏j=1NΓ(j/2)=G(N/2+1)⋅G(N/2+1/2)G(1/2). \underoverset {j = 1} {N} {\prod} \Gamma(j/2) \;=\; \frac { G(N/2+ 1) \cdot G(N/2 + 1/2) } { G(1/2) } \,.
