Definition

In homotopy type theory, a univalent groupoid or saturated groupoid is a univalent dagger category AA where for all objects a:Aa \colon A and b:Ab \colon A and morphisms f:Hom A(a,b)f \colon \mathrm{Hom}_A(a, b), f∘f †=id Bf \circ f^\dagger = \mathrm{id}_B and f †∘f=id Af^\dagger \circ f = \mathrm{id}_A. Equivalently, it is a univalent dagger category where every morphism is a unitary morphism.

Equivalently, a univalent or saturated groupoid is a precategory AA where for all objects a:Aa:A and b:Ab:A there is an equivalence between the identity type a= Aba =_A b and the type Hom A(a,b)\mathrm{Hom}_A(a, b):

a:A,b:A⊢disc(a,b):isEquiv(idToHom(a,b))a:A, b:A \vdash \mathrm{disc}(a, b):\mathrm{isEquiv}(\mathrm{idToHom}(a, b))

where

idToHom(a,b):(a= Ab)→Hom A(a,b) \mathrm{idToHom}(a, b) \;\colon\; (a =_A b) \; \to \; \mathrm{Hom}_A(a, b)
idToHom(x,y)(refl A(x))≔id A(x) \mathrm{idToHom}(x, y) \big( \mathrm{refl}_A(x) \big) \;\coloneqq\; \mathrm{id}_A(x)

The second definition implies that the only morphisms in AA are isomorphisms, making AA a groupoid, and thus the Rezk completeness condition holds in AA, making AA univalent.
