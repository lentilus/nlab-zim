The basic goal of this page is to turn Domenico’s and Urs’ statements about connections into statements about commutative diagrams.

The rough is idea is:

Higher Connection Flat≃Higher Diagram Commutes\text{Higher Connection Flat}\quad\quad\simeq\quad\quad\text{Higher Diagram Commutes}

Domenico: I guess this is well known, but let me try writing it here and see what happens.

If a connection on a principal G-bundle is locally represented by the 1-form ω\omega with values in 𝔤\mathfrak{g}, then the connection is flat if and only if the curvature 2-form F=dω+12[ω,ω]F=d\omega+\frac{1}{2}[\omega,\omega] vanishes, that is, if ω\omega is a solution of the Maurer-Cartan equation.

Now, what is interesting is that one can see curvature only on 2-dimensional paths (it is a 2-form): if one restricts ω\omega to a 1-dimensional submanifold, then there ω\omega is clearly a solution to the Maurer-Cartan equation. so, if I think of an infinitesimal 1-simplex and look at my connection there, I could say that my connection is 1-flat. then, moving to an infinitesimal 2-simplex I see that the connection is (generally) not 2-flat: holonomy along two sides of the 2-simplex is not the same thing as holonomy along the third side. not the same, but in a very precise way: the curvature FFFF exactly measures the gap to go from a horn of the 2-simplex to the third edge. this is very 2-categorical, and suggests I could cure the lack of flatness of my original connection by adding a copy of 𝔤\mathfrak{g} in degree -1 and cooking up a 2-Lie algebra 𝔩\mathfrak{l}. Maurer-Cartan equation for this 2-Lie algebra would coincide with the original one one the 1-simplex (since only degree 1 elements are 1-forms with values in 𝔩 0\mathfrak{l}_0. but on the 2-simplex we would have, in addition to these elements, also 2-forms with coefficients in 𝔩 −1\mathfrak{l}_{-1}, and the Maurer-Cartan equation on teh simplex would look like dω+12[ω,ω]+δF=0d\omega+\frac{1}{2}[\omega,\omega]+\delta F=0 where δ:𝔩 −1→𝔩 0\delta\colon \mathfrak{l}_{-1}\to \mathfrak{l}_{0} is the differential of the L ∞−algebra𝔩L_\infty-algebra \mathfrak{l} (and it should be induced by the identity of 𝔤\mathfrak{g}, thought as a degree 1 map from 𝔤[−1]\mathfrak{g}[-1] to 𝔤\mathfrak{g}). So the original equation telling that ω\omega had curvature FF is now equivalent to say that ω+F\omega+F is flat.

in other words, what seemed a non-flat connection was so since I was not seeing the 2-bundle, but only a 1-bundle approximation. and on a 1-bundle I can only clearly see up to 1-simplices, wher my connection was actually flat.

once curvature has come in, we can repeat the argument: now we have a 2-flat connection and test it on the 3-simplex. if it has 3-curvature, that will presumibly be because we are not seeing the 3-bundle, yet. so I find it natural to wonder (to conjecture) whether any connection on a principal bundle (and more generally any nn-flat connection on an n-bundle) can be seen as a flat connection on an ∞\infty-bundle.

Domenico: I think I could take the task of working a bit on oo-Lie algebroid valued forms in the direction sketched above. yet, I’d like to still discuss it a bit here, sicne I’m now thinking of some more radical change. what I’m thinking is that the one-step definition as it is now is too strict. I mean, we are now defining flat 𝔞\mathfrak{a}-connections as a morphism ∏ inf(X)→𝔞\prod^{inf}(X)\to\mathfrak{a}, and then general connections as something which becomes flat in a single step, i.e., going from 𝔞\mathfrak{a} to cone(𝔞cone(\mathfrak{a}). why should we restrict to this? it would be natural to consider thigs that become flat in 2 sterps, 3, steps, and so on. eventually we could have things which “become flat after infinity steps” (and by the way this vague notion seems to me to better fit with classical 𝔤\mathfrak{g}-connections).
