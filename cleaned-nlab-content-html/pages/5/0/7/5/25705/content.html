 Idea

In variation of the standard definition of topological spaces, a σ\sigma-topological space is a set XX equipped with a σ\sigma-topology: a collection O(X)O(X) of subsets of XX, called open sets, which is closed under

  1. finite\;intersections,

  2. countable\;unions.

(the difference being that for an ordinary topology one requires closure under arbitrary unions, see there).

The open sets O(X)O(X) of a σ\sigma-topological space form a σ \sigma -frame.

An example of a σ\sigma-topological space is a σ \sigma -algebra, whose collection O(X)O(X) is also closed under complements and countable intersections. Another example of a σ\sigma-topological space is a zero-set structure.

In constructive mathematics

In constructive mathematics, there may be non-trivial σ\sigma-subframes of the frame of truth values. As a result, in analogy with the case for admissible Archimedean ordered fields, let us say that given a σ\sigma-subframe of the frame of truth values Σ⊆Ω\Sigma \subseteq \Omega, a σ\sigma-topological space (X,O(X))(X, O(X)) is Σ\Sigma-admissible if and only if there is a function (−)∈ Σ(−):X×O(X)→Σ(-) \in_\Sigma (-):X \times O(X) \to \Sigma such that (x∈ ΣA)=⊤(x \in_\Sigma A) = \top if and only if x∈Ax \in A.

Assuming the limited principle of omniscience, the boolean domain is the initial σ\sigma-frame and simultaneously a σ\sigma-subframe of the frame of truth values. In addition, by definition of boolean-admissible σ\sigma-topological spaces, the open subsets are decidable subsets of the σ\sigma-topological spaces. As a result, the boolean-admissible σ\sigma-topological spaces are precisely the sigma-topological spaces found in classical mathematics.
