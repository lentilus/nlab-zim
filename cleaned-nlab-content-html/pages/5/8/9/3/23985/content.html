Idea

In dependent type theory, and particularly homotopy type theory, an identification is a word sometimes used for an inhabitant of an identity type. Alternatives to the term identification include identity, path, or equality, though those phrases are also used in different contexts.

Thus an identification p:Id(a,b)p:\mathrm{Id}(a, b) provides a “reason”, a “witness”, or a “proof” that aa and bb “are equal”, or more precisely a way in which to identify them. one can say that aa and bb are identified. The distinguishing feature of homotopy type theory is that in general, there may be more than one way to identify two things, i.e. more than one identification between two given elements.

Identifications of arbitrary arity

One also has identity types of arbitrary arity Id I,A(x¯)\mathrm{Id}_{I, A}(\overline{x}) indexed by x¯:I→A\overline{x}:I \to A given index type II, instead of the usual identity types for two elements Id A(x,y)\mathrm{Id}_A(x, y). The elements of these identity types are also called identifications, or identifications of arbitrary arity.

If the index type II is the standard finite type with nn elements Fin(n)≔∑ m:ℕm<n\mathrm{Fin}(n) \coloneqq \sum_{m:\mathbb{N}} m \lt n, then Id A(x¯)\mathrm{Id}_{A}(\overline{x}) is called the n-ary identity type and elements of said type can be called nn-ary identifications. For n=2n = 2, these result in the usual notion of binary identificaiton.
