Idea

The moment of a force.

 Definition

For point particles

Given a point particle PP in an Euclidean space ℝ n\mathbb{R}^n, an associated lever arm vector r→:ℝ→ℝ n\overrightarrow{r}:\mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}^n and a force f→:ℝ→ℝ n\overrightarrow{f}:\mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}^n acting on PP, the torque or moment of a force MM is the wedge product of the lever arm vector and the force:

M(t)=r→(t)∧f→(t)M(t) = \overrightarrow{r}(t) \wedge \overrightarrow{f}(t)

Thus, torque is a bivector.
