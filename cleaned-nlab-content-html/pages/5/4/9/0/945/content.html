Idea

In topology, a not necessarily continuous function f:X→Yf \colon X \to Y between Hausdorff spaces is dominant, or dense, in the sense that the image of ff is a dense subspace of YY, precisely if every continuous function g:Y→Zg \colon Y \to Z to any Hausdorff space ZZ is uniquely determined by the composition g∘fg \circ f.

In category theory, the concept of a dense functor is an analogy of this concept for functors.

An important special case that was also historically the source of the concept, is the case of a dense subcategory inclusion: a subcategory SS of category CC is dense if every object cc of CC is a colimit of a diagram of objects in SS, in a canonical way.

Remark

(Terminology and History)

Isbell 1960 introduced dense subcategories under the name left adequate subcategories. He showed that while the relation of being left (or right) adequate is not transitive, being adequate (left and right) is transitive. This brought out interesting connections with set theory and measurable cardinals.

Later, Ulmer 1968 considered the concept for more general functors F:C→DF:C\to D, not only inclusions I:C↪DI:C\hookrightarrow D, and introduced the name dense for them. In Gabriel & Ulmer 1971 this gave rise to the notion of locally presentable categories.

Compare also Lazard's criterion in the abelian context, due to Lazard 1964.

More recently, Jacob Lurie has referred to the analogue notion for (∞,1)-categories as strongly generating in a version (arXiv v4) of his HTT, but that term normally means something different.

Definition

Definition

(dense functor)
A functor i:S→Ci \colon S \to C is dense if it satisfies the following equivalent conditions:

  1. every object cc of CC is the colimit

    lim⟶((i/c)⟶pr SS⟶iC) \underset{\longrightarrow}{\lim} \Big( (i/c) \overset{\mathrm{pr}_S}{ \longrightarrow } S \overset{i}{\longrightarrow} C \Big)

    over the comma category (i/c)(i/c):

  2. every object cc of CC is the C(i−,c)C(i-,c)-weighted colimit of ii.

    (This version generalizes readily to the enriched category theory).

  3. the corresponding restricted Yoneda embedding C→[S op,Set]C \to [S^{op},Set] is fully faithful.

  4. the left Kan extension lan iilan_i i exists, is pointwise, and is isomorphic to the identity.

  5. CC is the closure of SS under colimits of a family of diagrams, by which is meant a class of pairs (F:L op→𝒱,P:L→C)(F \colon L^{op} \to \mathcal{V}, P \colon L \to C) consisting of a weight and a diagram for that weight, and these colimits are ii-absolute (i.e. preserved by the nerve N iN_i of ii), cf. Kelly 1982 Thm 5.19.

  6. For every category BB, precomposition by ii induces a full and faithful functor

    [i,1]:S-Cocts[C,B]→[S,B][i, 1] : S\text{-Cocts}[C, B] \to [S, B]

    where S-Cocts[C,B]S\text{-Cocts}[C, B] is the category of functors preserving weighted colimits whose weights are presheaves S op→VS^{op} \to V.

Properties

Remark

(dense functors not closed under composition)
Beware that the class of dense functors (Def. ) is not closed under composition of functors.

For a counter-example see Example below.

Examples

Example

The inclusion of the discrete category on the singleton set into all of Sets is a dense subcategory inclusion.

More generally, if CC is an essentially small category, then the Yoneda embedding C→[C op,Set]C \to [C^{op},Set] is dense.

Example

Let VV be a category of algebras and n∈ℕn \in \mathbb{N} such that VV has a presentation with operations of at most arity nn. Let vv be the free VV-algebra on nn generators. Then the full subcategory with object vv is dense in VV.

More generally, if VV is a κ\kappa-accessible category, then the full subcategory inclusion V κ⊆VV_\kappa \subseteq V of κ\kappa-presentable objects is dense. This means in particular that if CC is a small category, then the canonical inclusion C→Ind(C)C \to Ind(C) into its Ind category is dense, and that categories of sheaves have small dense subcategories.

Example

Consider the simplex category Δ\Delta, regarded in the usual way as a subcategory of Cat. Let Δ ≤[1]⊂Δ\Delta_{\leq [1]} \subset \Delta be the full subcategory with object set {[0],[1]}\{[0],[1]\} – i.e. comprising the 0-dimensional simplex and the 1-dimensional simplex.

Then

  1. Δ ≤[1]↪Δ\Delta_{\leq [1]} \hookrightarrow \Delta is a dense subcategory inclusion;

  2. Δ↪Cat\Delta \hookrightarrow \mathbf{Cat} is a dense subcategory inclusion (Kerodon 8.4.1.3: 03VB),

  3. but the composite Δ ≤[1]↪Δ↪Cat\Delta_{\leq [1]} \hookrightarrow \Delta \hookrightarrow Cat is not dense in Cat\mathbf{Cat} (see Remark ).

Example

The category TopTop of topological spaces does not have any small full subcategory which is dense. Indeed, TopTop is not generated under colimits by any small subcategory.

Example

The full subcategory of Set opSet^{op} on a countable set is dense if and only if there is not a proper class of measurable cardinals. (Isbell ‘60, Section 2.5.) In fact, Set opSet^{op} admits a small dense subcategory if and only if there is not a proper class of measurable cardinals. (Adámek–Rosický, Theorem A.5.)

Example

Let VV be a Bénabou cosmos. Then the inclusion of the 2-category of small VV-categories into the 2-category of large VV-categories is dense as a 2-functor (Lemma 2.16 of Przybyłek (2014)).

Example

In the category of schemes the subcategory of affine schemes is dense, see schemes as sheaves on affine schemes.

Example

In the category of smooth manifolds, the full subcategory whose objects are given by ℝ n\mathbb{R}^n for n≥1n\geq 1 with the standard smooth structure, is dense.

Example

In the category of modules over a ring RR, the full subcategory spanned by {R,R 2}\{R,R^2\} is dense.
