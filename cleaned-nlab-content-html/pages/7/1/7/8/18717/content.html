Idea

A categorification of the Dold-Kan correspondence appears in (Dyckerhoff17), where Ab, the category of abelian groups, is replaced by the (∞,2)-category of stable (∞, 1)-categories.

The equivalence between simplicial abelian groups and connective chain complexes from the ordinary Dold-Kan correspondence becomes an equivalence between 2-simplicial stable (∞, 1)-categories and connective chain complexes of stable (∞, 1)-categories.

This should lead to a categorified version of homological algebra and of cohomology, for example, through a categorified version of an Eilenberg-Mac Lane spectrum.

Categorified nerve functor

The nerve in the ordinary Dold-Kan correspondence sends a connective chain complex in an abelian category 𝒜\mathcal{A} to a simplicial object in 𝒜\mathcal{A}. The categorified nerve, 𝒩\mathcal{N}, is playing an analogous role on connective chain complexes of stable (∞, 1)-categories. This nerve unifies various known constructions from algebraic K-theory, in particular, for ℬ,ℬ 0,ℬ 1\mathcal{B}, \mathcal{B}_0, \mathcal{B}_1 all stable (∞, 1)-categories:

  • For ℬ[1]\mathcal{B}[1], the chain complex concentrated in degree 1, 𝒩(ℬ[1])\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{B}[1]) is a (∞,1)(\infty,1)-version of the Waldhausen S-construction.

  • For f:ℬ 1→ℬ 0f: \mathcal{B}_1 \to \mathcal{B}_0, then applying 𝒩\mathcal{N} to the corresponding chain complex concentrated in degrees 00 and 11 is an (∞,1)(\infty,1)-version of the relative Waldhausen S-construction.

  • For the chain complex concentrated in degree 22, 𝒩(ℬ[2])\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{B}[2]) is an (∞,1)(\infty,1)-version of Hesselholt-Madsen’s S • 2,1S^{2,1}_{\bullet}-construction. (See the reference at real algebraic K-theory.)

  • For the chain complex concentrated in degree kk, 𝒩(ℬ[k])\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{B}[k]) can be seen as a categorification of the Eilenberg-Mac Lane space N(B[k])N(B[k]).
