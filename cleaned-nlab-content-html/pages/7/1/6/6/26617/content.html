Definition

FieldField is the category with fields as objects and field homomorphisms as morphisms. It is a full subcategory of CRing, the more general category of commutative rings, which is more often considered. This is due to FieldField not being a very well-behaved category.

Properties

  • FieldField is neither finitely complete nor finitely cocomplete, meaning in particular, that it has neither products nor coproducts. (Riehl 17, p. 126) This implies that it is not locally presentable.
  • However, FieldField is a locally multipresentable category, which means in particular that it has connected limits and multicolimits.
  • The forgetful functor U:Field→SetU\colon Field\to Set does not have a left adjoint (hence FieldField is not a reflective subcategory of SetSet), hence there is no free field construction (contrary to many free functors for other algebraic categories). The same holds for weaker forgetful functors like U:Field→RingU\colon Field\to Ring, U:Field→AbU\colon Field\to Ab or the group of units − ×:Field→Ab-^\times\colon Field\to Ab. (Riehl 17, Example 4.1.11.)
  • Every morphism in FieldField is a monomorphism, hence FieldField is a left-cancellative category.
  • The isomorphisms in FieldField are the bijective homomorphisms. (Riehl 17, Examples 1.1.6. (ii))

Subcategories

FieldField is not connected as there are no field homomorphisms between fields of different characteristic. The connected component (full subcategory of FieldField) corresponding to characteristic pp (with p=0p=0 or pp prime) is denoted Field pField_p.

The field of rational numbers ℚ\mathbb{Q} is the initial object of Field 0Field_0 and the prime field 𝔽 p\mathbb{F}_p is the initial object of Field pField_p, but none are in FieldField, which has neither an initial nor terminal object. (Riehl 17, Examples 1.6.18. (vi))
