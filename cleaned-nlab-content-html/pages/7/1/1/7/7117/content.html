Idea

The concept of analytic spectrum is a realization of the concept of spectrum (geometry) in the context of non-archimedean analytic geometry. Given an affinoid algebra AA over a non-archimedean field, then the concept of spectrum of a commutative ring whose points are prime ideals/maximal ideals does not produce a sensible space that admits analytic geometry. Rather, instead of regarding points of the spectrum as ring homomorphisms to A→ℝA \to \mathbb{R}, the analytic spectrum instead takes points to be multiplicative seminorms |−|:A→ℝ ≥0{\vert -\vert} \colon A \to \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0} bounded by the norm on the given field.

If Spec anal(A)Spec_{anal}(A) is the set of all such multiplicative seminorms, then for x∈Spec anal(A)x \in Spec_{anal}(A) a point one writes the corresponding seminorm as |−| x{\vert - \vert}_x and thinks of it as being the norm on the function algebra on Spec(A)Spec(A) which is given by “evaluating functions at xx and then applying the field norm to that”.

One turns this Spec anal(A)Spec_{anal}(A) into a topological space in the usual way by choosing the weakest topology such that under this assignment the original elements of AA become continuous function on Spec anal(A)Spec_{anal}(A).

Globalizing this analytic spectrum construction leads to the concept of Berkovich analytic space.

Definition

For AA a normed ring, its analytic spectrum or Berkovich spectrum Spec analASpec_{anal} A is the set of all non-zero multiplicative seminorms on AA, regarded as a topological space when equipped with the weakest topology such that all functions

Spec analA→ℝ + Spec_{anal} A \to \mathbb{R}_+

of the form

x↦|x(a)| x \mapsto {\vert x(a)\vert}

for a∈Aa \in A are continuous.

If AA is equipped with the structure of a Banach ring, one takes the bounded multiplicative seminorms.

So a point in the analytic spectrum of AA corresponds to a non-zero function

‖−‖:A→ℝ {\Vert -\Vert} : A \to \mathbb{R}

to the real numbers, such that for all x,y∈Ax, y \in A

  1. ‖x‖≥0{\Vert x \Vert} \geq 0;

  2. ‖xy‖=‖x‖‖y‖{\Vert x y \Vert} = {\Vert x \Vert} {\Vert y \Vert};

  3. ‖x+y‖≤‖x‖+‖y‖{\Vert x + y \Vert} \leq {\Vert x \Vert} + {\Vert y \Vert}

and boundedness means that there exists C>0C \gt 0 such that for all x∈Ax \in A

‖x‖≤C|x| A. {\Vert x\Vert} \leq C {\vert x \vert}_A \,.

(e.g. Berkovich 09, def. 1.2.3)

Examples

Affine line

For kk a field and k[T]k[T] the polynomial ring over kk in one generator,

𝔸 k:=Spec analk[T] \mathbb{A}_k := Spec_{anal} k[T]

is the analytic affine line over kk.

If k=ℂk = \mathbb{C}, then 𝔸 k=ℂ\mathbb{A}_k = \mathbb{C} is the ordinary complex plane.
