Idea

There are many related constructions of algebras, topological algebras and so on which bear the name of a convolution algebra.

The basic mechanism is usually the

  • Convolution of maps from a coalgebra to an algebra .

The probably most widespread example of this is the

  • Convolution algebra of a group

This is a special case of the

  • Convolution algebra of a groupoid/category

Of maps from a coalgebra to an algebra

Let kk be a commutative unital ring, (C,Δ)(C,\Delta) a (counital) kk-coalgebra and (A,m)(A,m) an associative (unital) kk-algebra. Then the set of linear maps

Hom k(C,A) \mathrm{Hom}_k(C,A)

has a structure of an associative (unital) algebra, called convolution algebra, in which the product of two linear maps f,gf,g is given by

f⋆g=m∘(f⊗g)∘Δ.f\star g = m\circ(f\otimes g)\circ\Delta.

Of maps from a comonoid to a monoid in a closed monoidal category

Proposition

Let (𝒞,⊗,I,⊸)(\mathcal{C}, \otimes, I, \multimap) be a closed monoidal category, (A,Δ,η)(A,\Delta,\eta) a comonoid in 𝒞\mathcal{C} and (B,∇,ϵ)(B,\nabla,\epsilon) a monoid. Then A⊸BA \multimap B is a monoid.

Proposition

Let (𝒞,⊗,I,⊸)(\mathcal{C}, \otimes, I, \multimap) be a closed monoidal category, Mon(𝒞)Mon(\mathcal{C}) the category of monoids of 𝒞\mathcal{C} and Comon(𝒞)Comon(\mathcal{C}) the category of comonoids of 𝒞\mathcal{C}. We then have a functor:

Comon(𝒞) op×Mon(𝒞)→Mon(𝒞) Comon(\mathcal{C})^{op} \times Mon(\mathcal{C}) \rightarrow Mon(\mathcal{C})

which associate A⊸BA \multimap B to (A,B)(A,B)

Of a group

Given a finite group GG and a ring RR, the space of functions C(G,R)C(G,R) inherits the convolution product defined by

f 1⋆f 2:g↦∑ g 1⋅g 2=gf 1(g 1)⋅f 2(g 2). f_1 \star f_2 \;\;\colon\;\; g \;\mapsto\; \sum_{g_1 \cdot g_2 = g} f_1(g_1) \cdot f_2(g_2) \,.

This is the non-commutative product operation that appears in the Hopf algebra structure on C(G,R)C(G,R).

Of a groupoid/category
