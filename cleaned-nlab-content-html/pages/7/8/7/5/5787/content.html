In category theory

Definition

Given a regular cardinal κ\kappa, a morphism f:A⟶Bf \colon A \longrightarrow B in a category CC is κ\kappa-pure (or κ\kappa-universally injective) if for every commutative square

P ⟶g Q u↓ ↓v A ⟶f B \array{ P & \overset{g} {\longrightarrow} & Q \\ \mathllap{^u} \big\downarrow && \big\downarrow \mathrlap{^v} \\ A & \underset{f} {\longrightarrow} & B }

in which PP and QQ are κ\kappa-presentable objects, the morphism u:P→Au \colon P\to A factors through gg, i.e. there is some h:Q→Ah: Q\to A with u=h∘gu = h\circ g.

Remark

Def. does not require that also the morphism vv is factored, hence it does not express a lifting property.

Proposition

  • In a κ\kappa-accessible category CC every κ\kappa-pure morphism is a monomorphism, hence exhibits a pure subobject.

  • In a locally κ \kappa -presentable category κ\kappa-pure morphisms are, moreover, regular monomorphisms,

  • and in fact coincide with the κ\kappa-directed colimits of split monomorphisms in the arrow category C 2=Arr(C)C^2 = Arr(C);

  • more generally this characterization holds in all κ\kappa-accessible categories admitting pushouts.

(Adámek, Hub & Tholen 1996).

In ring theory and algebraic geometry

Definition

Given a unital, possibly commutative, ring ring RR, a homomorphism f:M→M′f \colon M\to M' of left RR-modules is pure in the sense of Def. if tensoring the exact sequence of left RR-modules

0→Kerf⟶M⟶fM′⟶Cokerf→0 0 \to Ker f \longrightarrow M \overset{f} {\longrightarrow} M' \longrightarrow Coker f \to 0

with any right RR-module NN (from the left) yields a exact sequence of abelian groups.

Remark

Beware that in algebraic geometry the category-theoretic terminology of Def. clashes with other terminology in a couple of ways:

  1. What are called pure morphisms of schemes (Raynaud & Gruson 1971) in algebraic geometry are not pure morphisms, according to Def. , in the category of schemes.

    Instead, a “pure morphism of schemes” f:X⟶Yf \colon X \longrightarrow Y is taken to be one whose corresponding morphism of structure sheaves 𝒪 Y⟶f *𝒪 X\mathcal{O}_Y \longrightarrow f_\ast \mathcal{O}_X is a pure morphism, in the sense of Def. , in the category of quasicoherent 𝒪 Y\mathcal{O}_Y-modules.

  2. What are called universally injective morphisms of schemes (or radicial morphisms (Grothendieck 1960, Stacks Project 01S2) are also not pure morphisms in the category of schemes according to Def. .

    Instead, a radicial morphism is one whose underlying continuous map remains injective after any base change.

Remark

Grothendieck has proved that faithfully flat morphisms of commutative schemes are of effective descent for the categories of quasicoherent 𝒪\mathcal{O}-modules. But this was not entirely optimal, as there is in fact a more general class than faithfully flat morphisms which satisfy the effective descent. For a local case of commutative rings, Joyal & Tierney (1984) have then proved (unpublished) that the effective descent morphisms for modules are precisely the pure morphisms of rings (or dually of affine schemes) according to Def .

This result can be extracted also from their Memoirs volume on Galois theory. Janelidze & Tholen (2004) have reproved this theorem as a corollary of a result for noncommutative rings obtained using Beck’s comonadicity theorem.

Descent along pure morphisms

The following paper, published as a CRAS note, was the first with the result on that pure morphisms are effective descent:

  • Jean-Pierre Olivier, Descente par morphismes purs, C. R. Acad. Sci. Paris Sér. A-B 271 (1970) A821–A823 scan on Gallica

The result is proved (within a larger context)

  • André Joyal, Myles Tierney, An extension of the Galois theory of Grothendieck, Mem. Amer. Math. Soc. 309 (1984) vol. 51, pages vii+71

  • Bachuki Mesablishvili, Pure morphisms are effective for modules, Applied Categorical Structures 21 (2013), 801–809. arXiv, doi.
