Definition

The notion of co-restriction of a function, or more generally of a morphism, is either the formal dual of that of restriction, or something similar. For the latter, recall:

Given any subset S⊂AS\subset A, we may consider the corresponding inclusion i S:S↪Ai_S \colon S \hookrightarrow A as a function. More generally, for a subobject S⊂AS\subset A (an equivalence class of monomorphisms in some ambient category) we may consider any representative monomorphism i S:S↪Ai_S \colon S\hookrightarrow A.

Then for any morphism f:A→Bf \colon A\to B, the restriction f| S:S→Bf|_S \colon S \to B of ff onto SS is the precomposition f| S≔f∘i Sf|_S \coloneqq f \circ i_S of ff by i Si_S. (For a different representative of the subobject, i S˜:S˜→Ai_{\tilde{S}} \colon \tilde{S}\to A there is a unique isomorphism b:S→S˜b \colon S\to\tilde{S} such that i S˜∘b=i Si_{\tilde{S}}\circ b = i_S, hence f S˜=f∘bf_{\tilde{S}} = f\circ b.)

This way, restriction of a morphism amounts to replacing its domain by one of its subobjects.

As factorization through the image

Similarly, the standard notion of corestriction (e.g. Böhm 08, Def. 3.1) of a morphism f:A⟶Bf \colon A \longrightarrow B is to instead replace the *co*domain BB by a subobject:

For a monomorphism i T:T↪Bi_T \colon T \hookrightarrow B, the corestriction f| T:A→Tf|^T \colon A \to T of ff onto TT is, if it exists, the unique morphism f| T:A→Tf|^T \colon A \to T such that there is a decomposition f=i T∘f| Tf = i_T \circ f|^T.

The corestriction exists if and only if TT contains the image of ff, that is, i Im(f)i_{Im(f)} factors through i Ti_{T}. In particular, by the universal property of the image, the corestriction onto the image S=Im(f)S = Im(f) always exists and this is sometimes understood as the corestriction of ff, by default.

As the formal dual of restriction

Alternatively, one may consider a notion of corestriction which is the formal dual to the notion of a restriction: if p U:B→Up^U \colon B\to U is an epimorphism, then the co-restriction of a morphism f:A→Bf \colon A\to B, in this dual sense, is the postcomposition p U∘f::A→Up^U \circ f \colon \colon A\to U of ff by p Up^U, which surely always exists.

This is the notion of corestriction considered e.g. in Andreotti 57, page 14, where the factorization through the image (above) is instead called coastriction.
