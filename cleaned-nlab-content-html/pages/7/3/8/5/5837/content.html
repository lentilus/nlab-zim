Definition

The diffeomorphism group Diff(X)Diff(X) of a smooth manifold XX is the group of its diffeomorphisms: the automorphism group of XX as an object of the category SmoothMfd.

This is generally a topological group. For XX compact, it is an infinite-dimensional Fréchet Lie group whose Lie algebra then is the algebra of vector fields on XX.

Beware that when XX is assumed orientable then sometimes, but not always, Diff(X)Diff(X) is implicitly taken to be the group of orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms.

In this vein, when XX carries further extra structure then one considers groups of diffeomorphisms which are automorphism preserving that extra structure. For instance

  • if XX is a G-manifold then one considers its equivariant diffeomorphism group,

  • if XX carries the structure of a Riemannian manifold then one considers its isometry group,

etc.

Properties

Relation to homotopy equivalences

For the following kinds of manifolds Σ\Sigma it is true that every homotopy equivalence

α:Π(Σ)⟶≃Π(Σ) \alpha \colon \Pi(\Sigma) \stackrel{\simeq}{\longrightarrow} \Pi(\Sigma)

(hence every equivalence of their fundamental infinity-groupoids) is homotopic to a diffeomorphism

a:Σ⟶≃Σ a \colon \Sigma \stackrel{\simeq}{\longrightarrow} \Sigma

i.e. that given α\alpha there is aa with

α≃Π(a). \alpha \simeq \Pi(a) \,.

  • for Σ\Sigma any surface (Zieschang-Vogt-Coldeway)

  • for Σ\Sigma a Haken 3-manifold (Waldhausen)

  • for Σ\Sigma any hyperbolic manifold of finite volume and of dimension ≥3\geq 3 (by Mostow rigidity theorem) (check)

Homotopy type and mapping class group

The homotopy type Π(Diff(Σ))\Pi(Diff(\Sigma)) of the diffeomorphism group Diff(Σ)Diff(\Sigma) is of interest (e.g. Hatcher 12).

For instance this is the automorphism ∞-group of a manifold, regarded as a k-morphism in an (∞,n)-category of cobordisms.

Specifically, the group of connected components is the mapping class group

π 0(Π(Diff(Σ)))=MCG(Σ). \pi_0(\Pi(Diff(\Sigma))) = MCG(\Sigma) \,.

For 1-manifolds

Π(Diff(S 1))≃Π(O(2)) \Pi(Diff(S^1))\simeq \Pi(O(2))
Π(Diff(D 1))≃Π(O(1)) \Pi(Diff(D^1))\simeq \Pi(O(1))

For 2-manifolds (surfaces)

Proposition

For Σ\Sigma a closed orientable surface, then the bare homotopy type ʃ(−)\esh(-) of its diffeomorphism group is

  1. if Σ\Sigma is the sphere then

    ʃ(Diff(S 2)) ≃Π(O(3)) ≃MCG(S 2)×Π(SO(3)) ≃ℤ 2×Π(SO(3)) \begin{aligned} \esh \, \big(Diff(S^2)\big) & \simeq \Pi\big(O(3)\big) \\ & \simeq MCG(S^2) \times \Pi\big(SO(3)\big) \\ & \simeq \mathbb{Z}_2 \times \Pi\big(SO(3)\big) \end{aligned}

  2. if Σ\Sigma is the torus then

    ʃ(Diff(S 1×S 1)) ≃MCG(S 1×S 1)×Π(S 1×S 1) ≃GL 2(ℤ)×B(ℤ×ℤ) \begin{aligned} \esh \, \big(Diff(S^1 \times S^1)\big) & \simeq MCG(S^1 \times S^1)\times \Pi(S^1 \times S^1 ) \\ & \simeq GL_2(\mathbb{Z}) \times B(\mathbb{Z} \times\mathbb{Z}) \end{aligned}

  3. in all other cases all higher homotopy groups vanish:

    ʃ(Diff(Σ))≃MCG(Σ). \esh \, \big(Diff(\Sigma)\big) \,\simeq\, MCG(\Sigma) \,.

(Where for the present purpose we are writing MCG(Σ)≔π 0Diff(Σ)MCG(\Sigma) \coloneqq \pi_0 Diff(\Sigma).)

The first statement is due to Smale 1959 (cf. also at sphere eversion). The second and third are due to Earle & Eells 67 and Gramain 73.

Similarly for surfaces with boundary:

Proposition

Let Σ\Sigma be a compact smooth surface with boundary, then the connected component of the topological subgroup Diff ∂Diff^\partial, of diffeomorphisms fixing the boundary pointwise, is contractible:

ʃDiff 0 ∂(Σ)≃1. \esh \, Diff_0^{\partial}(\Sigma) \;\simeq\; 1 \,.

(Earle & Schatz 1970, Thm 1D p 170)

The case of surfaces with punctures is handled by Yagasaki 2001.


For 3-manifolds

Proposition
ʃ(Diff(S 1×S 2))≃Π(O(2)×O(3))×ʃΩSO(3). \esh\big( Diff(S^1 \times S^2) \big) \;\simeq\; \Pi\big( O(2) \times O(3) \big) \times \esh \Omega SO(3) \,.

(Hatcher 81)

Theorem

(Smale conjecture)
The bare homotopy type of the diffeomorphism group of the 3-sphere is that of the orthogonal group O(4)O(4)

ʃ(Diff(S 3))≃ʃO(4)), \esh\big( Diff(S^3) \big) \;\simeq\; \esh \, O(4)) \,,

the equivalence being exhibited by the canonical inclusion

O(4)↪Diff(S 3). O(4) \hookrightarrow Diff(S^3) \,.

Also

ʃDiff(D 3)≃ʃO(3). \esh \, Diff(D^3) \;\simeq\; \esh \, O(3) \,.

After being conjectured by Smale, this was proven in (Hatcher 1983).

Generally:

Theorem

For every smooth 3-manifold the canonical map

Π(Diff(X))→Π(Homeo(X)) \Pi(Diff(X)) \to \Pi(Homeo(X))

sending diffeomorphisms to their underlying homeomorphisms of topological spaces is a weak homotopy equivalence.

That this follows from the Smale cojecture, theorem , was shown in (Cerf). For discussion see (Hatcher, 1978).

Proposition

If a 3-manifold XX is not a Seifert 3-manifold via an S 1S^1-action then

Π(Diff(X))≃MCG(X). \Pi(Diff(X)) \simeq MCG(X) \,.

If XX is Seifert via an S 1S^1-action, then the component of Diff(X)Diff(X) are typically Π(S 1)\Pi(S^1)-s.
