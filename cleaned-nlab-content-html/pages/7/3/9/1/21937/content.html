Idea

The quotient group ℚ/ℤ\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z} of the additive abelian group of rational numbers by its subgroup of integers.

It is also the coequalizer of the identity function of the rational numbers and the function x↦x+1x \mapsto x + 1 on the rational numbers:

ℚ⇉x↦x+1id ℚℚ→ℚ/ℤ \mathbb{Q}\underoverset{\quad x \mapsto x + 1 \quad}{\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{Q}}}{\rightrightarrows}\mathbb{Q} \to \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}

Properties

Relation to cyclic groups

For each natural number n∈ℕn \in \mathbb{N} the cyclic group ℤ/nℤ\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} is a subgroup

ℤ/nℤ ↪ ℚ/ℤ [k] ↦ [k/n] \array{ \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} &\overset{\;\;\;\;\;\;}{\hookrightarrow}& \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z} \\ [k] &\mapsto& [k/n] }

In fact ℚ/ℤ\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z} is the filtered colimit of a diagram of cyclic groups and inclusions between them. In more detail, whenever m|nm|n, multiplication by n/mn/m induces an inclusion

ℤ/mℤ↪ℤ/nℤ\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z} \hookrightarrow \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}

and this gives a functor to abelian groups from the lattice of natural numbers ordered by divisibility. The colimit of this functor is the abelian group ℚ/ℤ\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}.

Relation to Prüfer groups

For each prime pp the pp-Prüfer group ℤ[1/p]/ℤ\mathbb{Z}[1/p]/\mathbb{Z}, similarly formed as the colimit of groups ℤ/p kℤ\mathbb{Z}/p^k\mathbb{Z} and inclusions between them, embeds in ℚ/ℤ\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}. Furthermore, it follows from the Chinese remainder theorem? that the induced map

⨁ pℤ[1/p]/ℤ→ℚ/ℤ\bigoplus_p \mathbb{Z}[1/p]/\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}

is an isomorphism.

Relation to the circle group

We have a canonical subgroup inclusion into the circle group: If the latter is identified as the canonical subgroup U(1)⊂ℂ ×U(1) \;\subset\; \mathbb{C}^\times in the group of units of the complex numbers, this inclusion is given by

ℚ/ℤ ↪ U(1) [q] ↦ exp(2πiq). \array{ \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z} &\overset{\;\;\;\;\;\;}{\hookrightarrow}& U(1) \\ [q] &\mapsto& \exp\big( 2\pi\mathrm{i} q\big) \,. }

From this point of view, ℚ/ℤ\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z} is the torsion subgroup of U(1), whose elements are precisely the roots of unity.

Further properties

  • The group ℚ/ℤ\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z} is an injective object in the category Ab of abelian groups.

  • It is also a cogenerator in the category of abelian groups.

    Proof: let AA be an abelian group. It suffices to check that for every a∈Aa \in A there exists f:A→ℚ/ℤf: A \to \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z} such that f(a)≠0f(a) \neq 0. But if ⟨a⟩\langle a \rangle is the cyclic subgroup generated by aa, then it is easy to find a map g:⟨a⟩→ℚ/ℤg: \langle a \rangle \to \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z} such that g(a)≠0g(a) \neq 0, and then we can extend gg to a map f:A→ℚ/ℤf: A \to \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z} using injectivity of ℚ/ℤ\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}.

This means every abelian group embeds into an injective abelian group,

A→∏ f:A→ℚ/ℤℚ/ℤ,A \to \prod_{f: A \to \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z},

and into an algebraic double dual, A↪hom(hom(A,ℚ/ℤ),ℚ/ℤ)A \hookrightarrow \hom(\hom(A, \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}), \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}). The algebraic double dual of ℤ\mathbb{Z} is its profinite completion

∏ pℤ p\prod_{p} \mathbb{Z}_p

where ℤ p\mathbb{Z}_p is the group of pp-adic integers (this is connected with the direct sum decomposition of ℚ/ℤ\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z} into Prüfer pp-groups).

  • For any ring RR, its algebraic dual hom(R,ℚ/ℤ)\hom(R, \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}), equipped with the left RR-module structure defined by r⋅f:s↦f(sr)r \cdot f: s \mapsto f(s r), is an injective cogenerator in the category of left RR-modules. This follows easily from the corresponding property for ℚ/ℤ\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}.
