cleft extension

Definition

Let HH be a bialgebra, a right HH-comodule algebra EE is an HH-extension of a subalgebra U⊂EU\subset E if U=E coHU=E^{co H} is precisely the subalgebra of HH-coinvariants. The HH-extension U⊂EU\subset E is cleft if there exist a convolution-invertible HH-comodule map γ:H→E\gamma:H\to E. In other words, there is also a map γ −1:H→E\gamma^{-1}:H\to E (the convolution inverse of γ\gamma) such that for all h∈Hh\in H, γ(h (1))γ −1(h (2))=ϵ(h)=γ −1(h (1))γ(h (2))\gamma(h_{(1)})\gamma^{-1}(h_{(2)}) = \epsilon(h) = \gamma^{-1}(h_{(1)})\gamma(h_{(2)}).

Proposition

Every cleft HH-extension U↪EU\hookrightarrow E is a Hopf-Galois extension, namely the inverse of the canonical map E⊗ UE→E⊗HE\otimes_U E\to E\otimes H is given by e⊗h↦eγ −1(h (1))⊗ Uγ(h (2))e\otimes h\mapsto e\gamma^{-1}(h_{(1)})\otimes_U\gamma(h_{(2)}), where γ:H→E\gamma:H\to E is the cleaving map.

Any Hopf–Galois extension with the normal basis property is necessarily a cleft extension (Doi Takeuchi 1986, Theorem 9).
