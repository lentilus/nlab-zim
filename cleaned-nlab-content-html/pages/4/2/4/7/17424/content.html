Idea

The real projective space ℝP n\mathbb{R}P^n is the projective space of the real vector space ℝ n+1\mathbb{R}^{n+1}.

Equivalently this is the Grassmannian Gr 1(ℝ n+1)Gr_1(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}).

Properties

General

Proposition

Every continuous map ℝP n→ℝP n\mathbb{R}P^n\rightarrow\mathbb{R}P^n for nn even has a fixed point. This does not hold for nn odd as in this case the continuous map ℝP n→ℝP n,[x 0:x 1:…:x n−1:x n]↦[x 1:−x 0:…:x n:−x n−1]\mathbb{R}P^n\rightarrow\mathbb{R}P^n, [x_0:x_1:\ldots:x_{n-1}:x_n]\mapsto[x_1:-x_0:\ldots:x_n:-x_{n-1}] does not have a fixed point.

(Hatcher 02, page 155 exercise 2)

(Hatcher 02, page 180)

Proposition

For n≠1,3,7n\neq 1,3,7, the smallest number kk, so that there exists an immersion of real projective space ℝP n\mathbb{R}P^n into cartesian space ℝ k−1\mathbb{R}^{k-1}, is exactly the topological complexity TC(ℝP n)\operatorname{TC}(\mathbb{R}P^n) (with convention TC(*)=1\operatorname{TC}(*)=1).

(Farber & Tabachnikov & Yuzvinsky 02, Theorem 12)

Proposition

For n=1,3,7n=1,3,7 one has

TC(ℝP n)=n+1 \operatorname{TC}(\mathbb{R}P^n) =n+1

for the topological complexity (with convention TC(*)=1\operatorname{TC}(*)=1).

(Farber & Tabachnikov & Yuzvinsky 02, Proposition 18)

Cell structure

Proposition

(CW-complex structure)

For n∈ℕn \in \mathbb{N}, the real projective space ℝP n\mathbb{R}P^n admits the structure of a CW-complex.

Proof

Use that ℝP n≃S n/(ℤ/2)\mathbb{R}P^n \simeq S^n/(\mathbb{Z}/2) is the quotient space of the Euclidean n-sphere by the ℤ/2\mathbb{Z}/2-action which identifies antipodal points.

The standard CW-complex structure of S nS^n realizes it via two kk-cells for all k∈{0,⋯,n}k \in \{0, \cdots, n\}, such that this ℤ/2\mathbb{Z}/2-action restricts to a homeomorphism between the two kk-cells for each kk. Thus ℝP n\mathbb{R}P^n has a CW-complex structure with a single kk-cell for all k∈{0,⋯,n}k \in \{0,\cdots, n\}.

Homotopy groups

Proposition

(homotopy groups of real projective space)

The homotopy groups of real projective plane can be calculated with the long exact sequence of homotopy groups (Hatcher 02, Theorem 4.41.) of the fiber bundle S 0→S n→ℝP nS^0\rightarrow S^n\rightarrow\mathbb{R}P^n and are given by

(1)π k(ℝP n)={* k=0 ℤ k=1,n=1 ℤ 2 k=1,n>1 π k(S n) k≥1,n>0 \pi_k \big( \mathbb{R}P^n \big) \;=\; \left\{ \begin{array}{ll} \ast & k = 0 \\ \mathbb{Z} & k = 1, n = 1 \\ \mathbb{Z}_2 & k = 1, n > 1 \\ \pi_k \big( S^n \big) & k \geq 1, n > 0 \end{array} \right.

Homology and cohomology

Proposition

(homology and cohomology of real projective space)

The ordinary homology groups of real projective space ℝP n\mathbb{R}P^n can be calculated using its CW structure and are given by

(2)H k(ℝP n)={ℤ k=0ork=nifodd ℤ 2 koddand1≤k<n 0 otherwise H_k \big( \mathbb{R}P^n \big) \;=\; \left\{ \begin{array}{ll} \mathbb{Z} & k = 0 \quad or \quad k = n \quad if \quad odd \\ \mathbb{Z}_2 & k \quad odd \quad and \quad 1 \leq k \lt n \\ 0 & otherwise \end{array} \right.

(Hatcher 02, Example 2.42)

Similarly the ordinary cohomology groups of ℝP n\mathbb{R}P^n are

(3)H k(ℝP n)={ℤ k=0ork=nifodd ℤ 2 kevenand1≤k<n 0 otherwise H^k \big( \mathbb{R}P^n \big) \;=\; \left\{ \begin{array}{ll} \mathbb{Z} & k = 0 \quad or \quad k = n \quad if \quad odd \\ \mathbb{Z}_2 & k \quad even \quad and \quad 1 \leq k \lt n \\ 0 & otherwise \end{array} \right.

One has H n−1(ℝP n)≅ℤ 2H_{n-1}(\mathbb{R}P^n)\cong\mathbb{Z}_2 for nn odd and H n−1(ℝP n)≅1H_{n-1}(\mathbb{R}P^n)\cong 1 for nn even, hence ℝP n\mathbb{R}P^n is orientable iff nn is odd.

(Hatcher 02, Corollary 3.28.)

Relation to the ℤ/2\mathbb{Z}/2-classifying space

The infinite real projective space ℝP ∞≔lim⟶ nℝP n\mathbb{R}P^\infty \coloneqq \underset{\longrightarrow}{\lim}_n \mathbb{R}P^n is the classifying space B O ( 1 ) B O(1) for real line bundles. It has the homotopy type of the Eilenberg-MacLane space K(ℤ/2,1)=Bℤ/2K(\mathbb{Z}/2,1) = B \mathbb{Z}/2.
