Idea

The icosahedral group is the group of symmetries of an icosahedron.

As a symmetry group of one of the Platonic solids, the icosahedral group participates in the ADE pattern:

More in detail, there are variants of the icosahedral group corresponding to the stages of the Whitehead tower of O(3):

  • the full icosahedral group is the subgroup of O(3)

    I h↪O(3) I_h \hookrightarrow O(3)

    which is the stabilizer of the standard embedding of the icosahedron into Cartesian space ℝ 3\mathbb{R}^3;

  • the rotational icosahedral group I↪SO(3)I \hookrightarrow SO(3) is the restriction to orientation-preserving symmetries, hence to SO(3); this is isomorphic to the alternating group A 5A_5

  • finally the binary icosahedral group is the double cover (see at covering of alternating group), hence the lift of II to Spin(3)≃\simeq SU(2);

  • next there is a string 2-group lift ℐ↪String SU(2)\mathcal{I} \hookrightarrow String_{SU(2)} of the icosahedral group (Epa 10, Epa-Ganter 16)

Definition

Regard the icosahedron, determined uniquely up to isometry on ℝ 3\mathbb{R}^3 as a regular convex polyhedron with 2020 faces, as a metric subspace SS of ℝ 3\mathbb{R}^3. Then the icosahedral group may be defined as the group of isometries of SS.

Properties

General properties

The subgroup of orientation-preserving symmetries of the icosahedron is the alternating group A 5A_5 whose order is 60. The full icosahedral group is isomorphic to the Cartesian product A 5×ℤ 2A_5 \times \mathbb{Z}_2 (with the group of order 2).

Hence the order of the full icosahedral group is 60×2=120 60 \times 2 = 120 , as is that of the binary icosahedral group 2I2 I.

Proposition

There is an exceptional isomorphism

I≃PSL 2(𝔽 5) I \;\simeq\; PSL_2(\mathbb{F}_5)

of the icosahedral group with the projective special linear group over the prime field 𝔽 5\mathbb{F}_5.

and, covering this,

2I≃SL 2(𝔽 5) 2I \;\simeq\; SL_2(\mathbb{F}_5)

of the binary icosahedral group with the special linear group over 𝔽 5\mathbb{F}_5.

Proposition

The binary icosahedral group 2I2I is a perfect group: its abelianization is the trivial group.

In fact, up to isomorphism, the binary icosahedral group is the unique finite group of order 120 which is a perfect group.

Proposition

(quaternion group inside binary icosahedral group)

The binary icosahedral group contains the quaternion group of order 8, hence the binary dihedral group of order 8, as a subgroup (not normal):

2D 4=Q 8⊂2I. 2 D_4 =Q_8 \subset 2 I \,.

In fact the only finite subgroups of SU(2) which contain 2D 4=Q 82 D_4 =Q_8 as a proper subgroup are the exceptional ones, hence the binary tetrahedral group, the binary octahedral group and the binary icosahedral group.

See this Prop at quaternion group.

Proposition

(normal subgroups of binary icosahedral group

The only proper normal subgroup of the binary icosahedral group is its center Z(2I)≃ℤ/2Z(2I) \simeq \mathbb{Z}/2.
