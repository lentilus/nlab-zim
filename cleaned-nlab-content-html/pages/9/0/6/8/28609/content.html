Idea

The Cartan matrix of a complex semisimple Lie algebra 𝔤\mathfrak{g} (or of its root system) is the square matrix of integers (“Cartan integers”) that records the relative angles and lengths of a choice of simple roots α 1,⋯,α n\alpha_1, \cdots, \alpha_n:

a ij≔2(α i,α j)(α i,α i). a_{i j} \;\coloneqq\; \frac{ 2 (\alpha_i, \alpha_j) }{ (\alpha_i, \alpha_i) } \,.

Here we use the convention of Kac 1990; some authors (e.g. Humphreys 1972, §11.1) use the transpose.

For example, for type B 2B_2 (with α 1\alpha_1 long and α 2\alpha_2 short) the two conventions give, respectively,

(2 −1 −2 2)and(2 −2 −1 2). \left( \array{ 2 & -1 \\ -2 & 2 } \right) \;\;\;\; \text{and} \;\;\;\; \left( \array{ 2 & -2 \\ -1 & 2 } \right) \,.

The diagonal entries are all a ii=2a_{i i} = 2. The off-diagonal entries are in {0,−1,−2,−3}\{0, -1, -2, -3\}, with a ij=0a_{i j} = 0 iff a ji=0a_{j i} = 0. Up to the ordering of the simple roots, the Cartan matrix does not depend on the choice of simple roots (i.e. of a base Δ⊂Φ\Delta \subset \Phi in the sense of Humphreys 1972, §10.1), since the Weyl group acts transitively on these (Humphreys 1972, Thm. 10.3), nor on the choice of Cartan subalgebra, since all of these are conjugate (Humphreys 1972, Cor. 16.4). It is nonsingular, and it is equivalently encoded in the Dynkin diagram.

The Cartan matrix determines the root system up to isomorphism (Humphreys 1972, Prop. 11.1), and hence the Lie algebra up to isomorphism (Humphreys 1972, Thm. 18.4). In fact, 𝔤\mathfrak{g} is presented by generators e i,f i,h ie_i, f_i, h_i and the relations

[h i,h j]=0,[e i,f j]=δ ijh i,[h i,e j]=a ije j,[h i,f j]=−a ijf j [h_i, h_j] = 0 \,, \;\;\; [e_i, f_j] = \delta_{i j}\, h_i \,, \;\;\; [h_i, e_j] = a_{i j}\, e_j \,, \;\;\; [h_i, f_j] = - a_{i j}\, f_j

together with the Serre relations (ad e i) 1−a ij(e j)=0=(ad f i) 1−a ij(f j)(\mathrm{ad}_{e_i})^{1 - a_{i j}}(e_j) = 0 = (\mathrm{ad}_{f_i})^{1 - a_{i j}}(f_j) for i≠ji \neq j (Humphreys 1972, §18).

For example, the Cartan matrix of 𝔰𝔩(3)\mathfrak{sl}(3) (type A 2A_2) is

(2 −1 −1 2). \left( \array{ 2 & -1 \\ -1 & 2 } \right) \,.

More generally, a generalized Cartan matrix is any integer square matrix with a ii=2a_{i i} = 2, a ij≤0a_{i j} \leq 0 for i≠ji \neq j, and a ij=0⇔a ji=0a_{i j} = 0 \Leftrightarrow a_{j i} = 0. The same presentation then defines a Kac-Moody algebra (for symmetrizable matrices; in general one passes to a quotient), which is finite-dimensional precisely if the matrix is a Cartan matrix in the above sense (Kac 1990). Allowing in addition diagonal entries a ii≤0a_{i i} \leq 0 leads to Borcherds algebras.
