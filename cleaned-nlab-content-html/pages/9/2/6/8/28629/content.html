Idea

The Riesz formula expresses the projection onto the part of a linear operator that belongs to an isolated piece of its spectrum as a contour integral of the resolvent around that piece of the spectrum. The operator so defined is called the Riesz projection or Riesz projector.

This is the special case of the holomorphic functional calculus applied to a locally constant function which is 11 around the selected part of the spectrum and 00 around the rest.

Proposition

(basic properties)

In the situation of the above Definition:

  1. P σ *P_{\sigma_\ast} does not depend on the choice of contour Γ\Gamma, by the Cauchy integral theorem.

  2. P σ *P_{\sigma_\ast} is idempotent, P σ * 2=P σ *P_{\sigma_\ast}^2 = P_{\sigma_\ast}, and commutes with AA.

Proposition

(self-adjoint case)

If 𝒳\mathcal{X} is a Hilbert space and AA is self-adjoint (or just normal), then P σ *P_{\sigma_\ast} is an orthogonal projection, and it coincides with the spectral projection? 𝟙 σ *(A)\mathbb{1}_{\sigma_\ast}(A) obtained from the Borel functional calculus? of the spectral theorem.

Proposition

(smooth dependence on parameters)

If s↦A(s)s \mapsto A(s) is a C kC^k family of bounded operators (in the operator norm) such that the spectral splitting persists with a contour Γ(s)\Gamma(s) which may be chosen to depend continuously on ss, then s↦P σ *(s)s \mapsto P_{\sigma_\ast(s)} is also C kC^k, and the rank of P σ *(s)P_{\sigma_\ast(s)} is locally constant in ss.

This is a key ingredient in the quantum adiabatic theorem.

All of the above generalizes to closed unbounded operators AA, as long as σ *\sigma_\ast is bounded (e.g. Kato 1966, §III.6.4).
