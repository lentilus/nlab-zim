Definition

Definition

For ℋ\mathcal{H} a Hilbert space, the projective unitary group PU(ℋ)P U(\mathcal{H}) is the quotient of the unitary group U(ℋ)U(\mathcal{H}) by its center ZU(ℋ)≃U(1)Z U(\mathcal{H}) \simeq U(1), the circle group

PU(ℋ)≔U(ℋ)/U(1). P U(\mathcal{H}) \coloneqq U(\mathcal{H})/U(1) \,.

This is naturally a topological group. For ℋ\mathcal{H} of finite-dimension nn PU(n):=PU(ℋ)P U(n) := P U(\mathcal{H}) is also naturally a Lie group.

Properties

Homotopy type

Proposition

If ℋ\mathcal{H} is an infinite-dimensional separable Hilbert space the underlying topological space of its projective unitary group has the homotopy type of an Eilenberg-MacLane space K(ℤ,2)K(\mathbb{Z}, 2).

Proof

The unitary group U(ℋ)U(\mathcal{H}) in this case is contractible (by Kuiper's theorem) and the circle group U(1)U(1) acts free and faithfully on it. Therefore the quotient map U(ℋ)→PU(ℋ)U(\mathcal{H}) \to P U(\mathcal{H}) is a model for the universal principal U(1)-bundle and in particular the topological space underlying PU(ℋ)P U(\mathcal{H}) is equivalent to the classifying space BU(1)≃B 2ℤ≃K(ℤ,2)B U(1) \simeq B^2 \mathbb{Z} \simeq K(\mathbb{Z},2).

Proposition

For ℋ\mathcal{H} an infinite-dimensional separable Hilbert space PU(ℋ)P U(\mathcal{H})-principal bundles over a topological space XX are classidied by third integral cohomology of XX

PU(ℋ)Bund(X)≃H 3(X,ℤ). P U(\mathcal{H}) Bund(X) \simeq H^3(X, \mathbb{Z}) \,.

Proof

By prop. we have that the classifying space of PU(ℋ)P U(\mathcal{H}) itself is an Eilenberg-MacLane space

BPU(ℋ)≃BBU(1)≃B 3ℤ≃K(ℤ,3). B P U(\mathcal{H}) \simeq B B U(1) \simeq B^3 \mathbb{Z} \simeq K(\mathbb{Z}, 3) \,.

This is the classifying space for degree-3 integral cohomology (see Eilenberg-MacLane spectrum for more on this).

Proposition

Every circle 2-bundle/bundle gerbe on XX is equivalent to the lifting gerbe of some PU(ℋ)P U(\mathcal{H})-principal bundle to a U(ℋ)U(\mathcal{H})-bundle, and the equivalence classes of these structures correspond uniquely.

Proof

The twisted bundles of a given bundle gerbe are given by the twisted cohomology relative to the morphism BPU(ℋ)→B 2U(1)\mathbf{B} P U(\mathcal{H}) \to \mathbf{B}^2 U(1) that is part of the long fiber sequence

BU(1)→BU(ℋ)→BPU(ℋ)→B 2U(1). \mathbf{B} U(1) \to \mathbf{B} U(\mathcal{H}) \to B \mathbf{P} U(\mathcal{H}) \to \mathbf{B}^2 U(1) \,.

Since the topological space underlying U(ℋ)U(\mathcal{H}) is contractible, on the underlying topological spaces this is

K(ℤ,2)→*→K(ℤ,3)→≃K(ℤ,3). K(\mathbb{Z},2) \to * \to K(\mathbb{Z},3) \stackrel{\simeq}{\to} K(\mathbb{Z}, 3) \,.

This means that the morphism that sends PU(ℋ)P U(\mathcal{H})-bundles to the twist that they induce is an isomorphism.

Action on Fredholm operators

Let ℋ\mathcal{H} be an infinite-dimensional separable Hilbert space.

Since by the above PU(ℋ)≃BU(1)PU(\mathcal{H}) \simeq B U(1) and since there is a canonical action of line bundles on complex vector bundles, hence on the topological K-theory of a manifold XX, there must also be a natural action of PU(ℋ)×Fred→FredPU(\mathcal{H}) \times Fred \to Fred of PU(ℋ)PU(\mathcal{H}) on the space of Fredholm operators (on connected components).

This is given by letting a projective unitary act by conjugation on a Fredholm operator: (g,F)↦gFg −1(g, F) \mapsto g F g^{-1}.
