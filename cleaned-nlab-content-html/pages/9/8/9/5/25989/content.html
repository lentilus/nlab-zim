relative spectrum

Basic construction via gluing

If SS is a scheme and 𝒢\mathcal{G} is a sheaf of commutative 𝒪 S\mathcal{O}_S-algebras which are quasicoherent as sheaves of 𝒪 S\mathcal{O}_S-modules. For any affine U⊂SU\subset S, the spectrum of 𝒢(U)\mathcal{G}(U) is a UU-scheme. By gluing these spectra over all affine UU, one obtains an SS-scheme Spec(𝒢)Spec(\mathcal{G}), the relative spectrum of 𝒢\mathcal{G} over SS (also called the global spectrum of 𝒢\mathcal{G}).

An SS-scheme which can be obtained as a relative spectrum over SS is said to be a relative affine scheme.

Application to vector bundles and generalizations

If VV is a vector space, then the symmetric algebra Sym(V *)Sym(V^*) can be interpreted as the algebra of regular functions on VV considered as an (affine) space. Vector bundles in algebraic geometry are usually viewed as locally free sheaves of 𝒪\mathcal{O}-modules. However, one can construct their total spaces, using the symmetric algebra over the structure sheaf, obtaining a sheaf of algebras and then use the relative spectrum.

In more detail, let SS be an algebraic scheme and ℱ\mathcal{F} a quasicoherent sheaf of 𝒪 S\mathcal{O}_S-modules. Then the correspondence 𝒢:V↦Sym 𝒪 S(V)(ℱ(V))\mathcal{G}:V \mapsto Sym_{\mathcal{O}_S(V)}(\mathcal{F}(V)) for affine VV extends to a sheaf of 𝒪 S\mathcal{O}_S-algebras, hence one can apply the relative spectrum to obtain an SS-scheme Spec(Sym 𝒪 Sℱ)Spec(Sym_{\mathcal{O}_S}\mathcal{F}) which should be viewed as the total space of the “bundle” ℱ\mathcal{F} .
