Idea

What is called Verdier duality is the refinement of Poincaré duality from ordinary cohomology to abelian sheaf cohomology. The Grothendieck six operations formalism is a formalization of aspects of Verdier duality.

In general abstract formulation one says that a locally compact site XX (e.g. a locally compact topological space) satisfies Verdier duality if there exists a derived right adjoint 𝕃p !\mathbb{L}p^! (exceptional inverse image) to the operation ℝp !\mathbb{R}p_! of direct image with compact support for the terminal map X→*X \to \ast. More generally, in the relative situation, for f:X→Yf \colon X \to Y a map, Verdier duality of ff means that ℝf !\mathbb{R}f_! has a derived right adjoint. If here XX is a scheme and f !≃f *f_! \simeq f_\ast coincides with the direct image (“Grothendieck context”) then this is called Grothendieck duality. Therefore one often speaks of Verdier-Grothendieck duality.

If these adjoints exist, then one has the dualizing object in a closed category (dualizing module) ω≔𝕃p !k\omega \coloneqq \mathbb{L}p^! k (the image under the extra right adjoint of the unit object) and for every abelian sheaf VV on XX can define the dual object in a closed category

𝔻V≔[V,ω]. \mathbb{D}V \coloneqq [V,\omega] \,.

The analog of Poincaré duality is then the statement that the abelian sheaf cohomology with coefficients in VV is dual to the abelian sheaf cohomology with compact support with coefficients in 𝔻V\mathbb{D}V.

Implementations

A fairly general class of implementations of Verdier-Grothendieck duality is discussed in (Joshua, theorem 1.3).

With assumption (Joshua, 4.3) this derives that dualization intertwines the two pushforwards as 𝔻∘f *≃f !∘𝔻\mathbb{D} \circ f_\ast \simeq f_! \circ \mathbb{D} (Joshua, corollary 5.4).
