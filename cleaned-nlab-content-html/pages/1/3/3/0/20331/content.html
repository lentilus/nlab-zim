Idea

The quaternionic projective space ℍP n\mathbb{H}P^n is the space of right (or left) quaternion lines through the origin in ℍ n+1\mathbb{H}^{n+1}, hence the space of equivalence classes [q 1,⋯,q n+1][q_1, \cdots, q_{n+1}] of (n+1)-tuples of quaternions, excluding zero, under the equivalence relation given by right (or left) multiplication with non-zero quaternions

ℍP n≔{[q 1,⋯,q n+1]}≔({(q 1,⋯,q n+1)}∖{(0,⋯,0)})/ (q 1,⋯,q n+1)∼(q 1q,⋯,q n+1q)|q≠0 \mathbb{H}P^n \;\coloneqq\; \big\{ [q_1, \cdots, q_{n+1}] \big\} \;\coloneqq\; \Big( \big\{ (q_1, \cdots, q_{n+1}) \big\} \setminus \{(0, \cdots, 0)\} \Big) /_{ (q_1, \cdots, q_{n+1}) \sim (q_1 q, \cdots, q_{n+1} q) \vert q \neq 0 }

As nn ranges, there are natural inclusions

*=ℍP 0↪ℍP 1↪ℍP 2↪ℍP 3↪⋯. \ast = \mathbb{H}P^0 \hookrightarrow \mathbb{H}P^1 \hookrightarrow \mathbb{H}P^2 \hookrightarrow \mathbb{H}P^3 \hookrightarrow \cdots \,.

The sequential colimit over this sequence is the infinite quaternionic projective space ℍP ∞\mathbb{H}P^\infty. This is a model for the classifying space BSp(1)≅BSU(2)≅BSpin(3)B Sp(1) \cong B SU(2) \cong B Spin(3).

Properties

General

Proposition

Every continuous map ℍP n→ℍP n\mathbb{H}P^n\rightarrow\mathbb{H}P^n for n≥2n \geq 2 has a fixed point. This does not hold for n=1n = 1 as in this case ℍP 1≅S 4\mathbb{H}P^1\cong S^4 and the antipodal map S 4→S 4,x↦−xS^4\rightarrow S^4, x\mapsto -x does not have a fixed point.

(Hatcher 02, page 180)

Homology

Proposition

(homology of quaternionic projective space)

The ordinary homology groups of quaternionic projective space ℍP n\mathbb{H}P^n can be calculated using its CW structure and are given by

(1)H k(ℍP n)={ℤ | k=0,4,…,4(n−1),4n 1 | otherwise H^k \big( \mathbb{H}P^n \big) \;=\; \left\{ \array{ \mathbb{Z} &\vert& \; k = 0, 4,\ldots, 4(n-1),4n \\ 1 &\vert& otherwise } \right.

As a coset space

As any Grassmannian, quaternion projective space is canonically a coset space, in this case of the quaternion unitary group Sp(n+1)Sp(n+1) by the central product group Sp(n).Sp(1):

(2)ℍP n≃Sp(n+1)Sp(n)⋅Sp(1) \mathbb{H}P^n \;\simeq\; \frac{ Sp(n+1) }{ Sp(n)\cdot Sp(1) }

As a quaternion-Kähler symmetric space (Wolf space)

By the coset space-realization (2), quaternion projective space is naturally a quaternion-Kähler manifold which is also a symmetric space. As such it is an example of a Wolf space.

In string theory

  • Michael Atiyah, Edward Witten, p. 75 onwards in MM-Theory dynamics on a manifold of G 2G_2-holonomy, Adv. Theor. Math. Phys. 6 (2001) (arXiv:hep-th/0107177)
