Idea

A rigid (monoidal) category, also called an autonomous (monoidal) category is a kind of category with duals. In particular, a rigid monoidal category is a monoidal category in which every object AA has a dual object A *A^*.

Depending on whether these duals exist on the right or the left we call the category left rigid or right rigid. Sometimes rigid refers to categories with duals on both sides.

It is important to note that every right/left rigid category can be fully embedded into a category with duals on both sides, by repeatedly applying the double dual functor and taking a colimit (see e.g. Selinger 2011, Proposition 4.7).

Definition

A monoidal category is right rigid (or right autonomous) if every object has a right dual. Similarly, a monoidal category is left rigid (or left autonomous) if every object has a left dual. A monoidal category is autonomous (sometimes called rigid) if duals exist on both sides.

Conventions differ regarding which type of duals are which. One convention is as follows: a right dual of an object AA in a monoidal category 𝒞\mathscr{C} is an object A *A^* equipped with evaluation/co-evaluation maps

ev A:A⊗A *→1,coev A:1→A *⊗A\text{ev}_{A}:A\otimes A^*\to 1,\,\, \text{coev}_{A}:1\to A^*\otimes A

where 11 is the tensor unit, subject to the triangle identities (snake diagrams)

(ev A⊗id A)∘(id A⊗coev A)=id A,(\text{ev}_A\otimes \text{id}_A)\circ (\text{id}_A\otimes \text{coev}_{A})=\text{id}_{A},
(id A *⊗ev A)∘(coev A⊗id A *)=id A *.(\text{id}_{A^*}\otimes \text{ev}_{A})\circ ( \text{coev}_{A}\otimes \text{id}_{A^*})=\text{id}_{A^*}.

A left dual is an object A *A^* paired with morphisms ev A:A *⊗A→1\text{ev}_A:A^*\otimes A\to 1 and coev A:1→A⊗A *\text{coev}_A:1\to A\otimes A^* satisfying the dual conditions.

Examples

The canonical example of a rigid category is the category Vec k\text{Vec}_k of finite dimensional vector spaces over a field kk. Namely, given a vector space VV the standard dual V *=Hom k(V,k)V^*=\text{Hom}_k(V,k) serves as a categorical dual for VV. Explicitly we have the following:

Proposition

Let kk be a field. For every vector space V∈Vec kV\in \text{Vec}_k, define the maps

ev V:V⊗V *→k\text{ev}_V: V\otimes V^*\to k
(v,φ)↦φ(v)(v,\varphi)\mapsto \varphi(v)

and

x↦x⋅∑ i∈Iφ i⊗v ix\mapsto x\cdot \sum_{i\in I}\varphi_i\otimes v_i

where {v i} i∈I\{v_i\}_{i\in I} is a basis for VV, and φ i\varphi_i is a dual basis. That is, φ i\varphi_i is defined by

φ i(v j)={1 i=j 0 otherwise.\varphi_i(v_j)= \begin{cases} 1 & i=j\\ 0 & \text{otherwise}. \end{cases}

These formulas give well defined Vec k\text{Vec}_k-morphisms. Moreover, the satisfy the snake identities and hence give Vec k\text{Vec}_k the structure of a (right) rigid category.

Another key example is that of endofunctor categories. Given any category 𝒞\mathscr{C}, the hom-space of functors Fun(𝒞,𝒞)\text{Fun}(\mathscr{C},\mathscr{C}) is a monoidal category, with the monoidal structure coming from composition. Duals correspond to adjoint functor as can be stated formally below:

Proposition

Let 𝒞\mathscr{C} be a category, and let F∈Fun(𝒞,𝒞)F\in \text{Fun}(\mathscr{C},\mathscr{C}) be an endofunctor. Let F *F^* be the right adjoint of FF. F *F^* is a right dual for FF, under the natural transformations

ev F:F⊗F *→id 𝒞\text{ev}_{F}:F\otimes F^*\to \text{id}_{\mathscr{C}}
F(F *(A))→id F *(A)A,A∈𝒞F(F^*(A))\xrightarrow{\text{id}_{F^*(A)}} A,\,\, A\in \mathscr{C}

where we here make the implicit identification

Hom 𝒞(F(F *(A)),A)≅Hom 𝒞(F *(A),F *(A))\text{Hom}_{\mathscr{C}}(F(F^*(A)),A)\cong\text{Hom}_{\mathscr{C}}(F^*(A),F^*(A))

using adjointness, and

using adjointness. Conversely, if F *F^* is a right dual for an endofunctor FF then F *F^* is a right adjoint for FF.

Remarks

Note that this definition only asserts the existence of the dual objects. It does not assert that specific duals have been chosen. However, the choice of duals is unique up to unique isomorphism, justifying reference to ‘the’ dual of an object; in fact, this extends to a contravariant anafunctor *:M→M{}^*\colon M \to M. (Using the axiom of choice to pick duals for every object at once, we can make this into a strict functor.) This functor is defined explicitly by the formula

f *:A *→coev B⊗id A *B *⊗B⊗A *→id B *⊗f⊗id A *B *⊗A⊗A *→id B *⊗ev AB *f^*:A^*\xrightarrow{\text{coev}_B\otimes \text{id}_{A^*}} B^*\otimes B\otimes A^*\xrightarrow{\text{id}_{B^*}\otimes f\otimes \id_{A^*}} B^*\otimes A\otimes A^* \xrightarrow{\text{id}_{B^*}\otimes \text{ev}_A} B^*

This formula is clearer when written graphically:

Additionally, this definition does not assert that the right dual of an object is isomorphic to its left dual: this need not be the case in general, though it is true in a braided monoidal category, and thus automatically also in a symmetric monoidal category (this last fact can be considered an algebraic form of the “Whitney trick” for knots; see this MO discussion). Left duals and right duals are also isomorphic in a semisimple category. Note that a rigid monoidal category which is also symmetric is sometimes called compact closed, or simply “compact”.

In practice, algebraic geometers are the most frequent users of the term ‘rigid’, and they focus on the symmetric monoidal case, so they ignore the difference between right and left duals.

Tannaka duality

The statement of Tannaka duality for associative algebras says that rigid monoidal categories equipped with a fiber functor are categories of modules over a Hopf algebra.

Free rigid monoidal categories

The inclusion of the 2-category of monoidal categories into the 2-category of rigid monoidal categories admits a left 2-adjoint functor? LL.

Furthermore, the unit of the adjunction is a strong monoidal fully faithful functor, i.e., any monoidal category CC admits a fully faithful strong monoidal functor C→L(C)C\to L(C), where L(C)L(C) is a rigid monoidal category.

See Theorems 1 and 2 in Delpeuch.
