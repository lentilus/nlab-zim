Idea

In point set topology, an open map is a continuous map that sends open sets to open sets. The notion of an open geometric morphism is a generalization of this notion from topology to topos theory.

From a logical perspective, a geometric morphism f:ℱ→ℰf:\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{E} is open iff it preserves the interpretation of first order logic. This contrasts with general geometric morphisms which are only bound to preserve geometric logic.

Definition

A geometric morphism

f:=(f *⊣f *):ℱ→ℰ f := (f^* \dashv f_*) : \mathcal{F} \to \mathcal{E}

is called open if the following equivalent conditions hold

  • the localic reflection of ff over ℰ\mathcal{E} is an overt internal locale in ℰ\mathcal{E};

  • the inverse image f *f^* is a Heyting functor, hence preserves first order logic.

Examples

  • locally connected geometric morphisms are open. (cf. Johnstone (2002), p.650)

Properties

Proposition

A geometric morphism f:ℱ→ℰf:\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{E} is open iff the canonical map λ:Ω ℰ→f *(Ω ℱ)\lambda:\Omega_\mathcal{E}\to f_\ast(\Omega_\mathcal{F}) of poset objects in ℰ\mathcal{E} has an internal left adjoint μ:f *(Ω ℱ)→Ω ℰ\mu :f_\ast(\Omega_\mathcal{F})\to\Omega_\mathcal{E}.

(cf. Mac Lane-Moerdijk (1994), p.502)

Proposition

A geometric morphism f:ℱ→ℰf:\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{E} is open iff the pullback of any bounded geometric morphism with codomain ℰ\mathcal{E} is skeletal iff the pullback of any localic geometric morphism with codomain ℰ\mathcal{E} is skeletal.

This result appears as corollary 4.9 in Johnstone (2006).
